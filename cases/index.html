The short version: Most laser cases in court are about aircraft: aiming a laser pointer at a plane has been a federal crime in the US since 2012, and the FAA took more than 10,000 reports a year from 2023 to 2025. The record sentence, 14 years, came down to five after an appeal. Beyond the cockpit the docket gets thin and strange, with malpractice studies, a ruling on police laser speed guns, a treaty against blinding weapons and one reported settlement after a nightclub injury in Spain.

Is it a crime to shine a laser at a plane?#

In the US, yes, under two separate systems. The criminal one is 18 U.S.C. 39A, added by the FAA Modernization and Reform Act of 2012 (Public Law 112-95, section 311) on February 14, 2012. It makes it a crime to knowingly aim the beam of a laser pointer at an aircraft, or at its flight path, punishable by a fine, up to five years in prison, or both.223 The FAA puts the criminal maximum at five years and a $250,000 fine.458 The exceptions cover authorized research, development and flight testing, Defense and Homeland Security duties, and emergency distress signals.223

The civil one is the FAA's own. Relying on 14 CFR 91.11, the rule against interfering with a crewmember (applied to lasers in a June 1, 2011 legal interpretation), the agency can fine up to $11,000 per violation and has imposed up to $30,800 for multiple incidents.225 The FAA tells police that state and local agencies are often in the best position to investigate a strike.459

The volume is why all this exists. Reports to the FAA peaked at 13,304 in 2023, then ran 12,840 in 2024 and 10,993 in 2025 (official); the agency's table showed 5,997 for 2026 as of its October 2026 update, with the year still under way.225 By March 2025 the FAA counted 328 injuries since it began tracking reports in 2010, and California (1,489) and Texas (1,463) led the 2024 count.458 The year-by-year table and how outdoor shows coordinate with the FAA are in show laser rules.

What was the longest sentence for lasering an aircraft?#

Fourteen years, for a while. A federal jury in Fresno convicted Sergio Patrick Rodriguez in December 2013, and on March 10, 2014 he was sentenced to 14 years for aiming a laser pointer at a Fresno police helicopter in 2012. The helicopter had gone up to investigate reported strikes on an emergency transport helicopter for Children's Hospital of Central California.460 Prosecutors said it was believed to be the longest sentence in a laser-strike case and that the laser was 13 times more powerful than the permitted level for hand-held devices.460 "This is not a game. It is dangerous, and it is a felony," said US Attorney Benjamin B. Wagner. The same jury convicted his co-defendant, Jennifer Lorraine Coleman.460

The appeal is where the case became law. In United States v. Rodriguez, 790 F.3d 951 (9th Cir. 2015), the Ninth Circuit vacated his conviction and sentence on the interference count under 18 U.S.C. 32(a)(5) and (a)(8), and sent the 39A count back for resentencing.461 Courthouse News reported the reason as insufficient evidence that he wanted to bring the helicopter down, and that aiming at the helicopter just once would have been enough for a 39A offense.462

On resentencing he got 60 months, the 39A maximum, against an advisory guidelines range of 21 to 27 months. The Ninth Circuit affirmed on October 25, 2016 in an unpublished memorandum (No. 15-10495), pointing to six or seven strikes, a serious criminal history and the fact that he committed the offense while on probation.461 Courthouse News reported that Coleman was sentenced in 2014 to two years in prison, a sentence later revoked and replaced with five years' probation.462

How do other countries prosecute laser strikes?#

The UK charges them under the Laser Misuse (Vehicles) Act 2018, which reaches planes, trains, cars and boats and does not require proof of intent to endanger.457 On indictment the maximum is five years, a fine or both; the defenses are a reasonable excuse, or no intent plus all due diligence and reasonable precautions.456

Two recent cases, as police reported them. At Taunton Crown Court, Andrew Wilson admitted two counts under the Act, for lasering a helicopter over Yeovil on August 3, 2022 and an Airbus A320 descending toward Bristol Airport on August 14, 2022, and was sentenced on April 11 to four months' imprisonment, Avon and Somerset Police reported in April 2024.468 In Northern Ireland, Melvin Joyce pleaded guilty to shining a laser toward a moving helicopter and to recklessly or negligently endangering an aircraft, and was sentenced to four months in custody for the laser offense; the police release gives no year.469

Canada stacks its penalties. Possessing a hand-held laser over 1 mW in the restricted zones can bring a $1,000 fine under the Contraventions Regulations, or up to $5,000 for an individual and $25,000 for a corporation under the Canadian Aviation Regulations. Intentionally interfering with an aircraft by laser under the Aeronautics Act carries up to $100,000, five years, or both (Canadian dollars).230 Canada recorded nearly 250 reported laser strikes on aircraft in 2019 and 1,965 from 2015 to 2019 (official).230

Australia went after the supply instead: pointers over 1 mW have been prohibited imports since 2008. The rules country by country are in why the FDA regulates lasers.452

Has anyone sued over a concert laser?#

No US court record shows one. The cases that exist are overseas, and most are known second-hand through ILDA, the show laser trade association, which keeps a public incident list.470

The trade's own count for regular shows is small. ILDA estimates that documented injuries from continuous-wave visible show lasers "appear to be in the single digits" against about 140 million people and 14 billion exposures; pulsed lasers and audience pointers are outside that count (ILDA estimate).471 The medical detail on Aquamarine and other show injuries is in show safety.

Phones and cameras are a liability question with no court answer yet. ILDA's position is that "laser show producers cannot be responsible" for damage to audience cameras, and it suggests settling with the laser operator in advance who is responsible.206 It mentions a 2019 claim that a lidar unit at CES damaged a camera sensor, with no outcome given.206

What happens to companies that sell noncompliant lasers?#

The product cases are regulatory, not courtroom. In the US the main tool is Import Alert 95-04, which lets ports detain listed firms' laser pointers, projectors, gunsights and toys without examining them, over missing labels, output above 5 mW and missing reports.220 FDA's November 7, 2017 Safety Communication on show projectors recommended against using two brands, saying newer units had not been sold under an approved variance and some older ones might lack a manual reset and remote interlock connector. ILDA quotes FDA calling shows with the noncompliant units "potentially dangerous" and reports that such shows are prohibited under federal law. One maker answered three days later that it had hired third-party consultants to certify each product, and ILDA reports no final resolution.450

Canada uses consumer product law: hand-held pointers in Class 3B or 4 are treated as a danger and cannot be sold, imported, advertised or manufactured.451

What do laser malpractice cases look like?#

Medicine supplies the money cases, and the journals have counted them. A JAMA Dermatology study published online October 16, 2013 found 175 injury cases from cutaneous laser procedures between 1999 and 2012. Non-physicians operated in 42.9% of them, a share that rose from 36.3% in 2008 to 77.8% in 2011, and laser hair removal was the most common procedure.472

An update in Dermatologic Surgery (March 2022) covered 69 claims from 2012 to 2020. Non-physicians were the operators in 71%, hair removal was 64% of claims, burns appeared in 77% and scarring in 39%.385 Of 36 cases with a known outcome, 53% went for the plaintiff, and indemnity payments ran from $1,665 to $1.5 million, with a mean of $320,975 and a median of $132,108 (reported, nominal dollars).385

Eye surgery is a different pattern. A 2017 study in Cornea searched WestlawNext and found 167 refractive surgery cases, 127 of them LASIK.473 Of those, 35.3% ended unfavorably for the defendant, as did 23.6% of the 141 jury trials, and 49.7% alleged lack of informed consent. The inflation-adjusted mean award was $1,287,872; verdicts averaged $1,604,801 and settlements $826,883. Keratoconus after surgery and the need for further surgery made a plaintiff win more likely.473 How the treatments work is in lasers in medicine.

How have courts treated police laser speed guns?#

As reliable, with conditions. In a March 20, 1998 ruling, a New Jersey Superior Court judge in Morris County held that the LTI Marksman 20-20 laser speed detector produced "reasonably uniform and reasonably reliable measurements".474 Readings came in without expert testimony if the officer's training and the maker's pre-operational checks were shown, by day or night, in light or moderately heavy rain, up to 1,000 feet. Heavy rain, falling snow or longer range meant an expert was needed.474

The court's testing put a number on the error: the laser read more than 1 mph above the comparison device in 16 of 1,908 cases.474 The ruling finished a matter that opened with a June 1996 opinion, in which the same judge accepted laser speed measurement in principle but found the device not yet shown reliable enough.475474

Is there a treaty against blinding lasers?#

Yes. Protocol IV to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons, on blinding laser weapons, was adopted in Vienna on October 13, 1995 and entered into force on July 30, 1998.439 The UN treaty record lists 111 parties, and the United States consented to be bound on January 21, 2009, with an understanding on Article 2.439 What the protocol bans and what it leaves alone is in lasers in war, defense and space.

What laser cases are missing from the record?#

No FDA enforcement action against a named festival, show company or venue appears in an FDA primary source; the nearest thing is the 2017 Safety Communication on two projector brands, a product action rather than one against a show.450 No US lawsuit over a concert laser injury has turned up in court records, and the one reported settlement, in Spain, is known second-hand.470 No court has ruled on who pays when a show laser kills a phone camera.206 The FAA publishes its civil penalty ceilings but not how many penalties it has brought.225

Quick answers

Is it illegal to point a laser at a plane?
In the US, yes: 18 U.S.C. 39A, in force since February 14, 2012, makes knowingly aiming a laser pointer at an aircraft or its flight path a federal crime with up to five years in prison. The FAA can also impose civil penalties of up to $11,000 per violation.
How long can you go to jail for shining a laser at a helicopter?
The US statutory maximum under 39A is five years. In 2014 a Fresno man was sentenced to 14 years; after an appeal vacated his conviction on a second charge, he was resentenced to 60 months under 39A, which the Ninth Circuit affirmed in 2016. Recent federal sentences have ranged from nine days to 37 months.
Has anyone sued over a concert laser injury?
No US lawsuit over a concert laser injury appears in court records. ILDA's incident list reports an €80,000 settlement after a 2014 nightclub injury in Spain, known second-hand.
Can a laser at a concert break my phone camera, and who pays?
ILDA says camera sensors are generally more susceptible to laser damage than the eye and that show producers cannot be responsible for audience camera damage. No court has ruled on the question.

A history of the laser show, 1960 to 2000

The short version: The first laser fired in 1960, but it only flashed red; shows needed the steady, colored beams of the argon and krypton gas lasers that arrived in 1964. Artists were bending those beams through fog by the late 1960s, a composer and a physicist scanned them to music in 1969, and Laserium made the idea a ticketed night out in 1973. Rock tours picked lasers up in 1975, US regulators were at the shows by 1978, and by the late 1990s the hand-played analog console had mostly given way to a computer.

When did lasers become something you could build a show with?#

About four years after the first one worked. On May 16, 1960, Theodore Maiman and Irnee d'Haenens saw the first evidence of laser action from a ruby crystal at Hughes Research Laboratories, and Hughes told the press on July 7.31 The ruby laser gave red light at 694 nm in pulses, which is no use for drawing.32 The first gas laser, Ali Javan's helium-neon at Bell Labs on December 12, 1960, ran continuously but in the infrared, at 1.15 micrometers, where nobody can see it.3132 The red 632.8 nm helium-neon line came in 1962, and the Spectra-Physics Model 110 of that year was among the first commercial lasers with a continuous visible beam.32

Color arrived on a holiday. "It was Valentine's Day, February 14, 1964," William Bridges wrote of the day he first saw an argon-ion laser work at Hughes, with a blue line at 488 nm and others running to green.33 He had company: Guy Convert in France reported a blue line about a week before Bridges' letter, and groups at Yale and Spectra-Physics were working on it independently.33 Bell Labs had argon running continuously by May, at under 1 mW to begin with, and in July 1964 Bridges' group published 15 krypton lines.33 Krypton mattered because one tube can give red, yellow, green and blue at once.34

Raytheon was advertising a continuous argon laser by late 1964. Its 1 W LG-12 listed at $27,500 in a February 1965 brochure.33 A small helium-neon cost about $600 around 1970, by physicist Elsa Garmire's later recollection.35 Bridges' own list of what people did with ion lasers between 1964 and 1970 includes projection color TV and disco light shows.33 The tubes themselves are in the lasers chapter.

Who put on the first laser light show?#

It depends on what counts as a show, and almost every candidate comes with a second date.

Claim Who and when How solid
First laser art in a gallery Rockne Krebs, "Sculpture Minus Object", Washington Gallery of Modern Art, spring 1967 A historian's date. Wikipedia puts his first laser sculpture in Long Beach in 1968, and ILDA says his own exhibition list starts in 1968.363738
First laser on a theater stage Carl Frederick Reutersward, a Stockholm "Faust", 1968; Joel Stein, a ballet at the Opera Comique in Paris, about 1968 One trade history, unconfirmed elsewhere.39
First laser light show, full stop Joseph Stanislaus Ostoja-Kotkowski, "Sound and Image", Adelaide Festival of Arts, 1968 A lighting retailer's blog; a second account dates his first laser use in an opera production to 1974.4041
First multi-color, x-y scanned show Lowell Cross, David Tudor and Carson Jeffries, Mills College, May 9, 1969 Cross's own claim.42
First ongoing commercial show Laserium, Griffith Observatory, November 19, 1973 ILDA's wording, and the observatory confirms the date.3843

Krebs worked with red beams, artificial fog and mirrors, and in September 1969 filed what ILDA calls "apparently the first patent for laser artistic display".3638 In 1968 a Michigan inventor, Lloyd G. Cross, patented "Sonovision", a "visual display of sound", and offered a 2 mW version at $1,095.39

The strongest claim to a laser show in the modern sense belongs to a different Cross. On May 9, 1969, composer Lowell Cross, pianist David Tudor and physicist Carson Jeffries staged "Audio/Video/Laser" outdoors in the Greek Theatre at Mills College in Oakland, with a borrowed Coherent krypton laser. Cross later called it "the very first multi-color laser light show with x-y scanning."42 One trade history hedges that to "amongst the first true laser light shows".39

The Mills night was a rehearsal for a bigger job. Cross and Jeffries built a four-color krypton system for the Pepsi Pavilion at Expo '70 in Osaka, which opened on March 18, 1970 by one account and was dedicated on March 15 by Cross's.4442 About 2 million people passed through in six months, by Cross's count, though some colors were failing by April and nobody on site could maintain the laser.42 Garmire, who designed the pavilion's 90-foot mirror, found the show dull because it only drew Lissajous curves.45

The art world kept going. The Cincinnati Art Museum hosted "Laser Light: A New Visual Art" in December 1969, Willard Van De Bogart projected laser images on a 40 x 40 ft screen with the Los Angeles Philharmonic in 1971, and in Paris Iannis Xenakis's Polytope de Cluny opened in 1972 with three lasers, 600 flash lamps and networks of small mirrors.3946

What was Laserium?#

Ivan Dryer's Laserium opened in the planetarium at Griffith Observatory in Los Angeles on November 19, 1973 and stayed until January 2002.43 A laserist played each show live to recorded music.47 It was the first laser show most people could buy a ticket to, week after week.38 "Laserium was not the first laser light show, but it was the first to wow the public," Jeff Hecht wrote in 2021.45 The full story, conflicting numbers included, is in the planetarium chapter.

Which band used lasers on tour first?#

Led Zeppelin, by the dated evidence, and The Who, by their biographer. A Bruce Bennett photograph taken at Nassau Coliseum on February 13, 1975 shows three laser beams during "Dazed and Confused". Showco co-founder Jack Calmes remembered the laser as a 500 mW Coherent krypton and was unsure of the year, guessing 1974 or 1975.48 Zeppelin had a Showco laser of about 500 to 750 mW at Earls Court in London in May 1975, where John "Wiggy" Wolff of The Who saw it.49

Wolff went larger. On October 18, 1975, at Granby Halls in Leicester, The Who fanned a 4 W Spectra-Physics argon laser through a hand-held diffraction grating. The laser sat on a box at the back of the stage, cooled by a garden hose.4948 "The only way to get a bright laser was with a big 4W green argon," Wolff said.50 Who biographer Richard Barnes called it "the first time lasers had been used in a rock show anywhere". Circus magazine in March 1976, and the Zeppelin photograph, say otherwise. Rick Lefrak, speaking from The Who's side, conceded that Zeppelin "beat us" with a single beam.49 Zeppelin had a beam first; The Who had the first big multi-beam effect. Four small helium-neon projectors that Dryer and Charlie McDonald built for an Alice Cooper tour in 1972 predate both.51

Officials noticed at once. A Health and Safety inspector attended the Leicester show, and The Who were not allowed to use the lasers at the Wembley dates that followed.50 On December 30, 1976 the Evening Standard ran the headline "Laser Sparks Hitch For Genesis at The Rainbow", over a story about Greater London Council concerns.52 How modern tours carry lasers is in the stage chapter.

Did Blue Oyster Cult aim lasers at the audience?#

Yes. Blue Oyster Cult bought laser gear in 1976, after singer Eric Bloom saw lasers at Wings and Led Zeppelin shows, and sold it about 18 months later.53 On the Agents of Fortune tour a raw beam was put onto guitarist Buck Dharma's guitar and aimed into the audience, which ILDA's Patrick Murphy counts among the earliest audience scanning. Bloom recalled officials measuring light levels at several shows and said a rumored lawsuit over a blinded fan "never happened".54 Press summaries of a 1978 Washington Post report say no injuries were known.55

Which agency turned up depends on who is remembering. The band names OSHA.54 The press of 1978 names the Food and Drug Administration's Bureau of Radiological Health: Time reported that July that the bureau had begun sending inspectors to light shows, had told Blue Oyster Cult its show needed adjustment, and in June had halted a preview of an M.I.T.-sponsored show on the Mall in Washington.56 The Michigan Daily reported that the FDA had notified rock groups and laser makers of its standard the previous November, and quoted a spokesperson: "It never occurred to most rock groups that they'd ever have to worry about radiation".57 The bureau's policy statements on laser light shows were published to the industry in January 1979.58 What those rules became is in the rules chapter, and why they exist is in the safety chapter.

Who invented the laser harp?#

Bernard Szajner, on the usual account. The French artist, who had built light shows for Magma, Gong and The Who in the 1970s, created the instrument in 1980 and applied for a French patent in 1981.5960 An Australian, Geoffrey Rose, claims a UK provisional patent from 1975-76 and use as early as 1976.60

Jean-Michel Jarre made it famous. He ordered a harp from Szajner for his Concerts in China, five shows in Beijing and Shanghai in October 1981 that drew 218,000 people by the artist's official figure.5961 The instrument is a row of upright beams. Breaking one with a hand triggers a note.

Did disco have lasers?#

Some of it. Early-1970s New York DJs including Nicky Siano, Grandmaster Flowers and Pete "DJ" Jones brought their own laser lights to parties.62 A 1977 Billboard piece named David Infante, Blue Oyster Cult's laser designer, as the man also fitting out Studio 54.54 By the end of the decade a laser cost a club $10,000 to $75,000 and about 7% of US clubs had one, according to a 1980 trade book, and operators who fell short of the federal rules in 1979 faced fines of up to $1,000 and impoundment.62 Science Faction, founded in 1978, worked both the large concerts and the discos.63 The rest of that world is in the nightlife guide.

What were the big outdoor shows of the 1980s?#

Lasers left the dome and went up the side of a mountain. Stone Mountain, Georgia opened its laser show in 1983. Park officials estimated in 2009 that 20 million people had watched it in 25 years, with 8,000 to 20,000 on the lawn on a summer night.6465 A local guide calls it "the world's longest-running laser show", a title Laserium's 28 years at Griffith also claims, and says a "Light Show" replaced it in 2023.6667

EPCOT's "Laserphonic Fantasy" premiered on World Showcase Lagoon on June 9, 1984, with a central barge carrying a fiber-optic projection sphere, and ran until January 24, 1988.68 "IllumiNations" replaced it on January 30, 1988, with lasers on the tops of buildings around the lagoon. The name lasted until September 30, 2019.6970

The largest crowd belonged to Jarre. For Rendez-vous Houston on April 5, 1986, canvas sheets hung on downtown skyscrapers served as screens for lasers and projections timed to music and fireworks.71 Jarre's own site gives the attendance as "around 1.3 million" in its text and 1,500,000 in the header of the same page. Wikipedia, citing Billboard, says 1.5 million.7172 All are estimates.

Why was ILDA founded in 1986?#

The trade's own history does not say. The International Laser Display Association was founded in August 1986 at a meeting in Lake Tahoe, Nevada on August 15 and 16, followed by an East Coast meeting that November. Its first board drew on Laser Media, Coherent, Image Engineering, Audio Visual Imagineering and Science Faction, with Laser Media's Ron Goldstein as president.73 ILDA's history page carries no statement of purpose from the founders. Wikipedia lists the founding meeting's topics as business ethics, show quality, insurance, standardization, a market overview and the mission statement.74 The first recorded "lase-off", laserists showing off to each other, is dated the night before the meeting.73

What ILDA did next is on paper. Its technical committee wrote a common file format so frames could move between rival makers' systems, fixing a problem the specification describes as "colour #1 on one system might be black, while on another system it is white."75 The ILDA test pattern for tuning scanners was adopted on June 24, 1992.76 Both are explained in the programming chapter.

When did computers take over from the analog console?#

Gradually, from 1977. "Lovelight", at the Hayden Planetarium in Boston on February 4, 1977, is described as the first automated laser show, and the same year Jennifer Morris was among the first to draw representational pictures with a laser instead of abstract loops.3938 In 1978 Laser Displays was among the first to sell image synthesizers that stored pictures in PROM chips.39 The computers were not off the shelf. "When I started they were still building computers from scratch, literally," said Larry Alice, hired by Science Faction in 1982.63

Pangolin, by its own account, was founded in 1986 with a product called Laser PC and released Lasershow Designer 2000 in 1995.77 The mirrors caught up in the same years: between 1992 and 1996 scanner speeds nearly tripled, and the standard tuning rate rose from 12,000 to 30,000 points per second.78 By 1996 software could convert digitized photographs for laser output, crystals with no moving parts were mixing eight wavelengths, and mixed argon-krypton "white light" lasers put a balanced set of colors in one tube.78 The lasers were still gas. Laserium ran up to 20 W of argon-krypton in large venues, and one Seattle dome kept its krypton system until 2016.4541 How mirrors draw is in the scanning chapter.

Were there lasers at 1990s raves?#

Yes, though the record is a handful of sightings. The London club night Spectrum featured lasers in April 1988, and the first Energy rave, on May 27, 1989, had a laser rig for about 5,000 people. Laserium installed a show at a Los Angeles club night on September 7, 1990. A San Francisco promoter posted in December 1993 that a laser rental cost $300 a night, and the Organic '96 tour advertised "a 70-watt laser".62 Nobody has published a fuller account of who supplied rave lasers or what they ran.

Quick answers

Who invented the laser light show?
No single person. Rockne Krebs showed laser art in a gallery in the late 1960s, and Lowell Cross, David Tudor and Carson Jeffries staged a scanned, multi-color show to music at Mills College on May 9, 1969. Ivan Dryer's Laserium, which opened on November 19, 1973, was the first ongoing commercial laser show.
Which band was the first to use lasers in concert?
Led Zeppelin were photographed with three laser beams on February 13, 1975, and The Who used a 4 W argon laser through a diffraction grating that October. The Who's biographer calls theirs the first; the dated evidence puts Zeppelin earlier.
Who invented the laser harp?
Bernard Szajner created it in 1980 and applied for a French patent in 1981; an Australian, Geoffrey Rose, claims earlier work from 1975-76. Jean-Michel Jarre ordered one from Szajner and played it at his Concerts in China in October 1981.
What is ILDA and when was it founded?
The International Laser Display Association is the laser show industry's trade body. It was founded in August 1986 at a meeting in Lake Tahoe, Nevada, and went on to set a shared file format and a standard test pattern for tuning scanners.

## On stage: tours, festivals, companies and costs

**The short version:** Pink Floyd's 1994 US tour carried two 50 W laser systems; by 2025 the Guinness record stood at 1,100 separate laser sources in one stadium. The same short list of specialist companies turns up in the credits, and they publish gear lists far more readily than prices. Ticket prices, a US paperwork fee of zero and a few manufacturer list prices are public. Day rates, insurance and the size of the whole business are not.

### Which tours carried lasers, and what were the rigs?

Gear lists come from trade magazines, which print what suppliers tell them. For how a rig like this sits inside the rest of a production, see [how a dance music show is built](https://showfile.cold.fyi/).

- **Pink Floyd, 1994 US tour.** Lighting designer Marc Brickman had "2 complete, 50W, copper-vapor laser systems", built by Oxford Lasers and supplied by Rocklite of Toronto, with Warren "Wiggy" Toll operating and Allen Domanski programming. The Lighting Dimensions article that lists them survives online as a fan-archive transcript.[^biz-ld-1994]
- **Roger Waters, Dark Side of the Moon tour, 2007.** Partway through the tour, for the Mexico dates, Brickman added a rotating 3D prism drawn in the air by five direct-diode solid-state lasers totaling over 100 W, flown from cranes on a rig of roughly 2,500 lb. Lightwave International and Excitement Technologies Group built it; Martin Potoczny programmed and ran it on Pangolin software with grandMA playback. ILDA's Patrick Murphy told the trade press at the time: "Solid-state lasers are revolutionizing laser effects."[^biz-livedesign-waters]
- **Rush, Snakes & Arrows tour, 2007.** Two Laser Scope 60 W YAG lasers and two custom projectors from Production Design International, with Andrew Seabeck as laser tech. This list also survives as a fan-archive transcript, of a PLSN profile.[^biz-plsn-rush-2007]
- **Tool, 2024 US and Canada tour.** Lasers by Production Design International, which has worked with the band since 2009; Scott Wilson is credited as laser designer, programmer and operator.[^biz-plsn-tool-2025][^biz-plsn-lasers-2023]
- **Jean-Michel Jarre, 2017.** 13 Laser Image Supernova HD 35 W OPSL lasers, 16 RGB grating boxes and a 20 W **laser harp** - a fan of beams wired as a MIDI controller, so that breaking a beam with a hand triggers a synthesizer note. Hugo Bunk of Laser Image supplied the lasers, run on Pangolin Beyond and FB4.[^biz-plsn-jarre-2017] Jarre had been working at scale for decades: his Rendez-vous Houston concert on April 5, 1986 drew 1.3 million people by PLSN's count and 1.5 million by Wikipedia's, and Guinness listed it as the largest outdoor concert.[^biz-plsn-jarre-2017][^biz-wiki-houston]
- **Coldplay, Music of the Spheres.** X-Laser Triton 10 (10 W) and Triton 36 (36 W) units supplied through Strictly FX. Nobody has printed how many.[^biz-plsn-xlaser-2023]
- **The 2023 arena class.** PLSN's laser roundup that year listed The Weeknd's After Hours Til Dawn tour with several Kvant 25 W lasers from Pyrotecnico FX, programmed by Tyler Barbone; Drake with 10 ISFX 100 W lasers and six Kvant 30 W units from ImageSFX, four of them mounted on robots; and Aerosmith with 44 lasers of 30 W each planned. KISS's End of the Road tour, by Lightwave International's own figures, carried more than 200 lasers and 2,000 W.[^biz-plsn-lasers-2023]

Planetarium laser shows, their music licensing and the 2026 Phoebe Bridgers "Lost Weekend" run in 44 planetariums are a separate trade with separate economics, covered in [the planetarium chapter](/planetarium/).[^biz-billboard-2026]

### What is the biggest laser show ever?

It depends on what gets counted, and the counting unit has changed three times in eight years. The same shows sit alongside others in the [landmark shows table](/landmarks/).

| Date | Who | Where | Count | Unit counted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nov 18, 2017 | ER Productions | Outside the Las Vegas Convention Center, during the LDI trade show | 314, at 1,377 W in the closing sequence | laser fixtures[^biz-livedesign-record-2017][^biz-ilda-record] |
| Oct 17, 2019 | Image Engineering | Staples Center, Los Angeles | 642 | laser beams[^biz-ilda-record] |
| 2021 | Dubai Racing Club | Closing ceremony of the Dubai World Cup | 824 | as reported by Mixmag[^biz-mixmag-2025] |
| July 19, 2025 | Verknipt ArenA | Johan Cruijff ArenA, Amsterdam | 1,100 | laser emission sources[^biz-guinness-largest] |

The 2017 show was a seven-minute sequence, and it took about 13 tons of equipment and 6.8 miles of cable, with kit a Kent newspaper valued at £2.6 million.[^biz-cepro-2017][^biz-kentonline-2017] The 2019 record was certified on site by Guinness judge Andrew Glass.[^biz-ilda-record]

The current record is Guinness's "Largest laser show": 1,100 **laser emission sources** in front of about 40,000 people. Guinness counts each point where a beam leaves a unit, and a beam split by a diffraction grating counts once.[^biz-guinness-largest] The record piece ran 10 minutes.[^biz-mixmag-2025] The rig mixed RGB units above 30 W with single-color units and beam lasers, and the published safety measures read like a checklist: a risk analysis per device, a laser safety officer, compliance with DIN EN 60825-1, emergency stops, automatic beam interruption and sign-off by outside testers. The laser supplier has not been named.[^biz-pls-arena-2025]

Guinness keeps a second category for ground covered. That one belongs to "Pulse of Light" by Martin Arnaud at Noor Riyadh on November 15, 2022: 14,459,160 square meters.[^biz-guinness-area]

The FDA's own laser light show page lists the variance forms and no fee, and the industry guide warns that a new application "can require several months" of review.[^biz-fda-shows][^biz-ldi-guide] What the variance is and who needs one is in [the rules chapter](/rules/).

The only public worked example of how a laser company arrives at a day rate is an old one. L. Michael Roberts's LaserFX business pages, written between about 1996 and 2008, take a $50,000 system written off over 2.5 years: it has to earn about $20,000 a year, or roughly $1,000 per show-day at 20 shows a year. Add overhead and a 25% margin and his example lands near $2,750 per show-day, with later days at the same site billed at half.[^biz-laserfx-finances] It shows the arithmetic in dollars of that era; it is not a quote.

No vendor publishes a current day rate for a club laser with an operator, a festival laser package, a planetarium show lease or show-control software; they quote privately. Insurance premiums for laser shows are not published anywhere citable. Nobody has produced a credible estimate of the size of the show laser business. The number that circulates - a "laser projector" market of USD 15.5 billion in 2023, forecast to reach USD 69.9 billion by 2032 - is one research firm's press-release estimate, and it describes video projectors with laser light sources for cinemas, homes and classrooms.[^biz-gmi-2025] It says nothing about beams in haze.

### What is ILDA, and what are the ILDA Awards?

The **International Laser Display Association** is the trade body, founded at an organizational meeting in Lake Tahoe on August 16, 1986, with Ron Goldstein as president, Walt Meador as secretary and Tim Walsh as treasurer.[^biz-wiki-ilda] It has four membership categories - corporate, nonprofit, individual and student - and members get awards eligibility, conference access, free use of the Skyzan laser safety software, the member logo and recourse to an ethics committee.[^biz-ilda-benefits]

The ILDA Awards are the industry's annual prizes; how they are judged is in [the programming chapter](/programming/). The artistic categories include Beams/Atmospherics, Graphics, Abstract, Planetarium, Corporate, Live Stage, Live TV, Laser Photography and Laser Jockey. The **Fenning Award** goes to technical achievement, and the Career Achievement Award is the highest honor. In 2014 there were 204 entries from 38 member companies, a record number of entrants, and the ceremony was held in Las Vegas on November 19.[^biz-ilda-awards-2014] The first Career Achievement Award went to Laserium's founder Ivan Dryer; ILDA's own page gives the year as both 1988 and 1989.[^biz-ilda-laserium]

ILDA also runs an accreditation, the **ILDA Accredited Professional Lasershow Company**. A company needs at least 15 documented shows over two years, an award or two award entries, three client references, an employee who has passed a Laser Safety Officer course and a signed safety affirmation. ILDA membership is not required. Five companies hold it: Lightwave International, LOBO electronic, Laser Production Network, Laser Spectacles and Dynamic Lasers.[^biz-ilda-accreditation] It is an industry credential, separate from any government permission.

### Is laser art in galleries a different thing from a laser show?

Same light, different job.

**Rockne Krebs** (1938-2011) moved from Plexiglas sculpture to laser light in 1967 with "Sculpture Minus Object" at the Washington Gallery of Modern Art: red beams, fog and mirrors, and nothing else in the room. He worked with Hewlett-Packard physicists in 1969, showed in the US pavilion at Expo '70 in Osaka, and went on to make more than 30 large public light works.[^biz-mccray-2015] In September 1969 he filed US patent 3,622,228, which ILDA calls apparently the first patent for laser artistic display. His 1983 piece "The Green Hypotenuse" ran a single beam 7 miles from Mount Wilson to Caltech.[^biz-ilda-history]

**Hiro Yamagata** put "Photon 999" on the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao from October 31 to December 2, 2001: 15 lasers projecting onto a structure on the facade, with a walkway through the light.[^biz-guggenheim-photon] The beams, from tarm Showlaser, Times of Change and Dymax, were aimed at more than 200 mirrors on 20 m truss towers.[^biz-lsi-photon]

**Yvette Mattern**'s "Global Rainbow" is seven parallel beams in the colors of the spectrum, first shown in New York in 2009 and since taken to Berlin, three UK sites for the London 2012 Cultural Olympiad, Toronto's Nuit Blanche in 2014 and Pittsburgh, where Lightwave International supplied the lasers.[^biz-publicdelivery-rainbow][^biz-wiki-mattern]

**Chris Levine**'s "Higher Power" sent one green 515 nm beam from San Clemente island across Venice from May 4 to 11, 2026, during the Biennale's opening week.[^biz-designboom-levine] **Rita McBride**'s "Portal", at the 2016 Liverpool Biennial, crossed beams inside the Toxteth Reservoir; the projector maker Kvant says the piece used 16 green modules of 1 W each at 520 nm.[^biz-kvant-art]

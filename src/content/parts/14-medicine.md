## Lasers in medicine and beauty

**The short version:** Medicine picked up the laser almost the moment it existed: a ruby laser treated a patient's retinal tumor in New York in November 1961, a year after the first laser worked. Eyes came first because the eye is a window, and a beam can reach the retina without a cut. Skin came next, once a 1983 paper showed how to heat one target - pigment, blood, ink - and leave the tissue around it alone. Today the same physics runs from LASIK and glaucoma clinics to hair removal, tattoo removal, dentistry, cancer drugs switched on by light and home caps sold for thinning hair, each with its own FDA path and its own quality of evidence.

### Who first used a laser on a patient?

On November 22, 1961, at Columbia-Presbyterian Medical Center in New York, Dr. Charles J. Campbell of the Edward S. Harkness Eye Institute destroyed a retinal tumor, an angioma, with a single ruby laser pulse lasting about a thousandth of a second.[^med-nyp-ruby] Dr. Charles Koester of American Optical Co., which supplied the laser, worked with him.[^med-nyp-ruby] The same year, Zaret and colleagues made the first laser lesions in an eye, in the iris and retina of rabbits.[^med-stanford-palanker] A 2011 history of eye lasers in *Archives of Ophthalmology* cites Campbell's clinical photocoagulation through a 1965 paper and does not mention the 1961 case; the hospital's own history leads with the 1961 patient.[^med-stanford-palanker][^med-nyp-ruby]

The idea was older than the laser. In 1949 Gerhard Meyer-Schwickerath published on coagulating the retina with sunlight, then moved to carbon and xenon arc lamps.[^med-asrs-retina] **Photocoagulation** - heating tissue with light until it seals or dies - was already an eye-clinic technique. The laser made it smaller and aimable.

### Who was Leon Goldman, and why is he called the father of laser surgery?

Leon Goldman (1905-1997) was experimenting with lasers in his research lab shortly after the first one worked in 1960.[^med-derm-goldman] He performed his first laser surgery in 1966, ran the Laser Treatment Center of the Jewish Hospital in Cincinnati from 1980, and was the founding president of the American Society for Laser Medicine and Surgery.[^med-derm-goldman] The Dermatology Hall of Fame calls him the "father of laser surgery".[^med-derm-goldman]

### How did an IBM lab and a Thanksgiving turkey lead to eye surgery?

The day after Thanksgiving in November 1981, IBM researcher Rangaswamy Srinivasan brought leftover turkey into the lab, and the team aimed an **excimer laser** - an ultraviolet gas laser that removes tissue in very thin layers with little heat - at cartilage still attached to the bone.[^med-ibm-excimer] The discovery is credited to James Wynne, Srinivasan and Samuel Blum.[^med-ibm-excimer] Wynne tried the laser on his own left pinky and reported no heat and no pain.[^med-ibm-excimer]

In 1983 Trokel, Srinivasan and Braren made clean excimer incisions in corneas that had been removed from eyes.[^med-stanford-palanker] The first corrective laser eye surgery on a sighted person followed in March 1988.[^med-ibm-excimer] IBM puts the total at about 60 million people treated with laser vision correction worldwide since 1990, a company figure.[^med-ibm-excimer]

### What is the difference between PRK, LASIK and SMILE?

All three reshape the cornea, the clear front of the eye, so it focuses differently. They differ in how the laser reaches the tissue.

| Procedure | What happens | First FDA approval on FDA's list | Laser |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRK** (photorefractive keratectomy) | The excimer laser reshapes the corneal surface directly, with no flap | March 10, 1995: Summit Technology excimer laser system, PMA P910067[^med-fda-prk-list] | Excimer (ultraviolet) |
| **LASIK** | A thin flap is lifted, the excimer reshapes the cornea underneath, and the flap is laid back down | July 30, 1998: Kremer Excimer Laser System, LaserSight Technologies, PMA P970005[^med-fda-lasik-list] | Excimer; the flap can be cut by a femtosecond laser |
| **SMILE** (small incision lenticule extraction) | A femtosecond laser cuts a small disc of tissue inside the cornea, and the surgeon removes it through a small incision | September 13, 2016: Zeiss VisuMax, PMA P150040[^med-fda-prk-list] | Femtosecond |

Pallikaris and colleagues coined the word "LASIK" in 1991, and in 1998 Kurtz and colleagues reported cutting corneal flaps with a **femtosecond laser**, one whose pulses last quadrillionths of a second.[^med-stanford-palanker]

FDA's list of approved LASIK lasers starts on July 30, 1998, and FDA's risks page also says the first LASIK approval came in 1998.[^med-fda-lasik-list][^med-fda-lasik-risks] IBM's history says 1997.[^med-ibm-excimer]

The VisuMax approval covers certain nearsighted patients aged 22 and over. In its trial, 328 patients were evaluated at six months: 88% saw 20/20 or better without correction, and all but one saw 20/40 or better.[^med-fda-visumax] FDA's listed complications include difficulty removing the tissue, loss of suction, debris, dry eye, and moderate to severe glare and halos.[^med-fda-visumax]

### How many people get LASIK now?

The most recent published US counts date from 2011, in a trade-journal article by the president of the market research firm Market Scope: about 1.4 million procedures a year in 2000 and again in 2007, falling to about 800,000 a year around 2011.[^med-crst-harmon]

### What does FDA say about LASIK risks?

FDA's patient page lists risks without percentages: some patients lose vision that glasses, contacts or more surgery cannot fix; glare, halos and double vision; poorer night vision; under- or over-correction; severe dry eye that may be permanent; flap problems, inflammation and infection; and worse results for very large prescriptions.[^med-fda-lasik-risks] It adds that "Long-term data are not available".[^med-fda-lasik-risks] It flags "20/20 vision or your money back" offers and package deals, and says, "There are never any guarantees in medicine."[^med-fda-lasik-risks]

FDA approves the lasers, not the surgeons. "FDA does not regulate the practice of medicine and does not have a registry of doctors," its FAQ says; problems go to its **MedWatch** reporting system or to a state medical board.[^med-fda-lasik-faq]

On July 27, 2022, FDA issued draft guidance on LASIK patient labeling. It recommends expanded labeling, a patient decision checklist handed over before surgery that covers who is a good candidate and the long-term risks, and images showing the visual symptoms that can follow.[^med-aao-draft-labeling]

### How common are halos and dry eye after LASIK?

The FDA-led PROWL studies, published in *JAMA Ophthalmology* in January 2017, followed 534 people: active-duty US Navy personnel in PROWL-1 and civilians in PROWL-2.[^med-aao-prowl] Within three months, 43% and 46% reported a visual symptom they had not had before - double images, glare, halos or starbursts.[^med-aao-prowl] About 28% of people with normal dry-eye scores before surgery had mild, moderate or severe dry-eye symptoms at three months.[^med-aao-prowl] At the same time, only 1-4% were dissatisfied with their vision and 1-2% with the surgery.[^med-aao-prowl]

The symptom figures are self-reports at three months, not a count of lasting complications, and a person can notice halos and still be glad they had the operation.

### What do lasers do for the retina?

The argon laser, discovered by Bridges in 1964, gave the retina its workhorse: L'Esperance reported a large clinical series in 1969, and Coherent began selling a slit-lamp argon photocoagulator in 1970.[^med-stanford-palanker] Trials set the rules for its use: Diabetic Retinopathy Study Report 8, on photocoagulation for proliferative diabetic retinopathy, in 1981, and Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study Report 3 in 1987.[^med-asrs-retina] Anti-VEGF drugs injected into the eye later reduced the laser's role in wet macular degeneration.[^med-asrs-retina]

### Is laser treatment for glaucoma as good as eye drops?

Glaucoma lasers have a long lineage: laser iridectomy (Beckman and Sugar, 1973), argon laser trabeculoplasty (Wise and Witter's pilot, 1979) and **selective laser trabeculoplasty**, or SLT, introduced by Latina and colleagues in 1995.[^med-stanford-palanker] SLT treats the eye's drainage tissue to lower pressure.

The LiGHT trial, published June 4, 2019, randomized 718 adults with newly diagnosed open angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension at six UK hospitals to SLT first or drops first.[^med-nihr-light] After three years, 74.2% of the laser group needed no drops to stay at their target pressure.[^med-nihr-light] The trial estimated a saving of GBP 451 per patient in specialist eye care costs (2019 figure).[^med-nihr-light]

### What is selective photothermolysis, and what did it make possible?

In 1983 John A. Parrish and R. Rox Anderson published **selective photothermolysis** in *Science*: choose a wavelength the target absorbs and a pulse shorter than the time it takes heat to leak out of that target, and the heat stays in the pigment, the blood vessel or the ink while the skin around it is spared.[^med-mdedge-derm]

| Target | Laser | When | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood vessels: port-wine stains | **Pulsed dye laser** (yellow light) | Tan's NEJM report on 35 children, 1989; on the market in the early 1990s[^med-mdedge-derm] | Treats port-wine stains without scarring[^med-mdedge-derm] |
| Hair | Ruby laser | 1990s (Anderson and Melanie Grossman)[^med-mdedge-derm] | Heats the pigment in the hair follicle |
| Tattoo ink | **Q-switched** lasers (nanosecond pulses) | About 15 years before picosecond lasers[^med-mdedge-derm] | Shatters ink so the body can clear it |
| Tattoo ink, benign pigmented lesions | **Picosecond** laser (PicoSure) | FDA-cleared by January 2, 2013[^med-lfw-picosure] | Shorter pulses for the same job |
| Skin surface | CO2 and erbium YAG with scanners | Early 1990s[^med-mdedge-derm] | Computer-controlled resurfacing |
| Skin, in a grid of columns | Fractional (Fraxel) | FDA-cleared in the early 2000s[^med-mdedge-derm] | **Fractional resurfacing** leaves untreated skin between columns to speed healing |

Dermatologist Mathew Avram calls hair removal the "most common laser treatment in medicine", an expert's view rather than a counted figure, and calls CO2 lasers "the gold standard for facial rejuvenation" that need skilled hands.[^med-mdedge-derm]

### Who is allowed to do laser hair removal?

It depends on the state, and the rules can cover "who can own the machine, to who can develop the treatment plan, to finally, who can actually fire the laser".[^med-amspa-laws] FDA regulates the laser as a device; many states regulate the person through general medical practice laws enforced by medical boards.[^med-amspa-laws] A trade-association explainer sets Texas against Georgia and California to show how "requirements can vary drastically", without giving the other states' details.[^med-amspa-laws]

Texas runs a dedicated program, and what follows is Texas law alone. Anyone performing laser hair removal needs a certificate from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, at one of four levels: Apprentice-in-Training, Technician, Senior Technician and Professional.[^med-tdlr-faq] Each facility needs a written contract with a **consulting physician**, buying a device takes a physician's order, licensed health professionals answer to their own boards, and the Department of State Health Services handles the radiation safety of the devices.[^med-tdlr-faq]

### Can laser hair removal burn you?

The published injury figures come from lawsuits. A study of skin laser litigation from 1999 to 2012 found 175 cases, 42.9% involving non-physician operators; that share rose from 36.3% in 2008 to 77.8% in 2011, and hair removal was the most common procedure.[^med-sciencedaily-jalian] In 2008-2012, 85.7% of hair removal suits involved non-physicians.[^med-sciencedaily-jalian] The author, H. Ray Jalian, said unsupervised laser work is legal in many states at non-medical sites such as medical spas.[^med-sciencedaily-jalian]

An update covering 2012 to 2020 found 69 liability claims: 64% involved hair removal and 71% non-physician operators.[^med-asds-khalifian] Burns appeared in 77% of claims, scarring in 39%, pigment changes in 23% and blistering in 12%.[^med-asds-khalifian] The authors suspect underreporting, and they write that patient safety appears to improve when physicians, particularly dermatologists, do the work.[^med-asds-khalifian]

These are cases that reached a court or a claim, a small and skewed slice of all treatments. Neither study gives a rate of burns per treatment.[^med-asds-khalifian]

### How are lasers used in dentistry?

FDA's general category for surgical lasers names dentistry among the specialties where lasers cut, remove or coagulate mostly soft tissue.[^med-fda-gex] A 2019 position statement from the American Association of Endodontists says lasers were proposed for dentistry about 35 years earlier and that FDA has cleared various lasers for gum and other soft tissue work, removing decay, tooth-colored restorations and as an add-on in root canal treatment.[^med-aae-dental]

The same statement is cool on the root canal claims. Laser shaping of the canal has not been shown to beat mechanical shaping, the heat can injure dentin, soft tissue and bone, and the effects of laser disinfection "on the clinical outcomes of root canal therapy are not known at this point".[^med-aae-dental]

### How do lasers treat cancer and brain disease?

Photodynamic therapy (PDT) pairs a light-activated drug, a **photosensitizer**, with light of a matching wavelength; the drug, once lit, makes a form of oxygen that kills the cells that absorbed it.[^med-nci-pdt] The light gets through only about 1 cm of tissue, so PDT is limited to tumors on or just under the skin or on the lining of organs.[^med-nci-pdt] The National Cancer Institute lists FDA-approved uses for actinic keratosis, advanced cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, Barrett esophagus, basal cell skin cancer, esophageal cancer, non-small cell lung cancer and stage 0 squamous cell skin cancer, plus relief of symptoms from tumors blocking the esophagus or airway.[^med-nci-pdt] Side effects include burns, swelling, pain and scarring.[^med-nci-pdt]

Photofrin (porfimer sodium), first approved in the US in 1995, is lit with 630 nm laser light 40 to 50 hours after injection.[^med-drugs-photofrin] NCI says skin and eyes stay light-sensitive for about six weeks; the drug's label says to avoid sunlight and bright indoor light for at least 30 days, with sensitivity lasting "up to 90 days or more" in some patients.[^med-nci-pdt][^med-drugs-photofrin]

**Laser interstitial thermal therapy** (LITT) works from the inside: a laser fiber placed in the brain heats and destroys tissue while MRI tracks the temperature in real time.[^med-medtronic-litt] Medtronic's Visualase, which uses lasers in the 800-1064 nm range, is FDA-cleared to ablate epileptic foci and brain tumors, including radiation necrosis; the maker says neurosurgeons have used it since 2007.[^med-medtronic-litt]

### Do laser hair-growth caps and red light actually work?

**Low-level light therapy** (LLLT, also called photobiomodulation) uses low-power red or near-infrared light meant to stimulate tissue rather than cut it. The first FDA-cleared LLLT device for male pattern hair loss arrived in 2007, and as of January 5, 2020, 32 home-use devices were FDA-cleared and on the US market, mostly emitting red light around 650 nm (range 620-678 nm).[^med-jcad-lllt]

The evidence is thinner than the shelf. A 2021 meta-analysis could pool only seven double-blind randomized trials, 607 people in all, found significant variation between them, saw no follow-up beyond 26 weeks and no head-to-head device comparisons, and noted that dosing was reported inconsistently; its own confidence intervals for the pooled result do not match between abstract and results.[^med-jcad-lllt] A 2016 review of 21 studies, only five of them randomized trials, called the cleared devices safe and effective for pattern hair loss in people who had not responded to or could not tolerate standard treatments, while noting that only one trial used intention-to-treat analysis and only one other clearly described its randomization.[^med-pubmed-zarei]

"FDA-cleared" means the device went through 510(k) as substantially equivalent to one already sold, not through the premarket approval review used for LASIK lasers. Plenty of sources say "FDA-approved" anyway, including the title of the 2021 meta-analysis.[^med-jcad-lllt][^med-fda-gex]

### Can a laser pointer damage a child's eyes?

Case reports say yes. A 2016 series in *Pediatrics* described four boys aged 9 to 16 with macular injuries from laser pointers, three with possibly irreversible vision loss; the authors called for limits on children's use, education and possibly laws on sale and use.[^med-pediatrics-pointer] A six-year series from Northern Ireland, published in *Eye* in 2022, described 65 children (74 eyes) with macular injuries from handheld lasers.[^med-eye-reid] Their mean age was 11.6, 72% were male, and only 40% had symptoms.[^med-eye-reid] Outer retinal holes carried 3.5 times the risk of significant vision impact and damage to the external limiting membrane 3.4 times, though overall vision loss was low and more than half the eyes that arrived with impaired vision improved.[^med-eye-reid]

These are case series. They describe what the injuries look like, not how often they happen. FDA's 2023 consumer update says laser pointers are not toys, that they have grown more powerful, that eye injuries "often do not cause immediate pain" and vision can fade over days, and that toys should stay within IEC Class 1; it also notes that aiming a laser at an aircraft is a federal crime.[^med-fda-laser-toys]

### How does a medical laser reach the US market?

Most surgical and cosmetic lasers fall under 21 CFR 878.4810, "laser surgical instrument for use in general and plastic surgery and in dermatology": Class II, product code GEX, reviewed by FDA's General and Plastic Surgery panel and cleared through **510(k)**, the route for devices shown to be substantially equivalent to one already on the market.[^med-fda-gex] The definition covers cutting, destroying, removing or coagulating mostly soft tissue across many specialties.[^med-fda-gex]

Refractive lasers took the harder road. The excimer and femtosecond systems for PRK, LASIK and SMILE went through **premarket approval** (PMA), which requires clinical evidence of safety and effectiveness - hence PMA numbers like P910067, P970005 and P150040 on FDA's lists.[^med-fda-prk-list][^med-fda-lasik-list]

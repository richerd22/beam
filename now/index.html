Laser shows now: cheap diodes, fireworks and what is next

In this cue

The short version: Between about 2005 and 2014 the laser show moved off water-cooled gas tubes and onto solid-state and then diode sources, and rigs went from a pair of lasers to hundreds. Cities have begun using lasers and drones where fireworks used to go, mostly for reasons of fire, smoke and noise. The rules for buying and running a show laser have barely moved: in the US, anything above 5 mW of visible output still needs an FDA variance, and the regulator keeps a standing import alert on projectors that arrive without one.

How did diode lasers change the price and size of a laser show?#

In 2010, hobbyists got their first affordable high-power blue laser diodes by taking apart Casio data projectors. One hobbyist's account, relayed by a laser safety site, has a "harvester" paying about $800 for a projector and selling its 24 diodes at $46 each.295 The 445 nm diode inside was being made in volume as a light source for video projectors, and volume pushed the price down.122

One projector maker's timeline gives the sequence for show lasers: water-cooled gas lasers through the 1990s; DPSS (diode-pumped solid-state) lasers becoming the default around 2005, with prices dropping sharply; diode projection proving itself between 2010 and 2014; and practical 520 nm green diodes around 2014, which made an all-diode red, green and blue projector possible.296 The maker gives no dollar figures, and nobody has published a clean price-per-watt series for those years. How each of these lasers makes its light is in the lasers chapter.

A krypton gas system for a laser dome cost $100,000 in the 1970s. Seattle's Laser Dome ran one until 2016, then replaced it with a digital system of 10 laser projectors.41 In November 2026 the projector maker Kvant's page for a 10 W club projector carried a price of 8,582 EUR before VAT in its metadata, while the page a visitor sees said "price on request".16

Size and count moved the same way. Pink Floyd's 1994 US tour carried two 50 W copper-vapor laser systems, a gear list that survives as a fan transcript of a trade article.234 In 2007 Roger Waters's tour flew five solid-state lasers totaling over 100 W from cranes.235 By 2026 a single touring projector was rated at 58 W or 60 W, and a residency at Sphere in Las Vegas used 20 of the 58 W units, with a row of 60 W units across the stage front on top of that.245 The Guinness record for the number of lasers in one show went from 314 fixtures in 2017 to 1,100 emission sources in 2025.253252 The rigs and the records are in the stage chapter.

The newest hardware trick is variable divergence: a projector that can widen or narrow its beam during the show. Kvant's BeamBrush units were on the 2024 Super Bowl halftime show and on Coachella stages in 2025, by the account of the company whose control software ran them.243244

Are laser shows replacing fireworks?#

In some cities, for some holidays, and usually for a reason that has nothing to do with lasers being prettier.

City When What replaced the fireworks Reason given
Mexico City New Year's Eve 2018 A concert and laser show on Reforma Avenue Air pollution297
Flagstaff, Arizona July 4, 2023 A free laser show set to music Extreme fire risk298
Boulder, Colorado July 4, 2023 Drones Fire danger298
Tahoe City before 2024 Drones Wildfire risk299
Napa and Salt Lake City July 4, 2024 Drones Not stated299
Nicosia New Year 2026 Drones Children, pets, the elderly, people with autism or PTSD; no debris or fire risk300
Athens New Year 2026 "Quiet" fireworks, lights and drones The same300

Drones took most of those slots, with lasers the smaller share. The health argument is about what a firework leaves in the air: an air-quality researcher told ABC News that fireworks release metal particles that can worsen heart and lung conditions.298 The animal argument is mostly about sound. Wildlife researcher Bill Bateman, on what bothers animals at public displays: "it's less the light and more the noise".299

The criticism is that a quiet show is not the same thing. In Mexico City many of the people who came out said they missed the fireworks.297 Katie Biggers, in Tahoe City, put it this way: "Fireworks are something that just hits all of us."299 A Canadian fireworks trade group, asked about Vancouver's fireworks-free New Year, said drone shows cost more and that fireworks "are felt with the nose, the eyes, the senses".301

Lasers and fireworks also share a bill. The FDA's laser light show page lists use alongside fireworks among the ordinary settings for a laser display.28

Is that beam in the sky a laser or a searchlight?#

If it is a tight colored line that stays pencil-thin for miles, it is probably a laser. If it is a fat white shaft sweeping the clouds over a car dealership, it is probably a lamp.

The lasers are easy to name when an artist signs them. Yvette Mattern's "Global Rainbow" is seven parallel laser beams, one per color of the spectrum, first shown in New York on January 19, 2009 and later in Berlin, Toulouse, Utrecht, Nantes, three UK sites for the London 2012 Cultural Olympiad, New Haven, Pittsburgh, Derry, Leuven, Cleveland and Toronto.302 How far it can be seen depends on who is asked: an art site says "up to 35 miles", and the laser supplier says "more than 60 km" for Nantes in 2011. Neither figure was measured.273303 Chris Levine's "Higher Power" over Venice in May 2026 was a single green 515 nm laser beam, and Rockne Krebs ran one 7 miles from Mount Wilson to Caltech in 1983.27438

A laser carries because it barely spreads. A physics news service worked the example for a 5 mW pointer: at 100 m the spot is about 100 times wider than where it started, and from an aircraft at 40,000 ft in clear air it would still look about as bright as a quarter moon.11

The classic searchlight is a xenon lamp in a reflector. The line between the two is blurring from the lamp side: Claypaky's Skylos, reported in 2023, keeps the searchlight housing and puts a 300 W white laser source inside it. It draws 600 W, is rated IP66, has a beam adjustable from 0.5 to 5 degrees, and the maker claims output equal to a 4,000 W xenon lamp.23

Any beam aimed at the sky is also an aviation matter. US pilots reported 10,994 laser strikes in 2025, down 14 percent from 12,840 in 2024 and the second yearly fall in a row, with 337 pilot injuries reported since tracking began in 2010.224 Those reports are overwhelmingly about handheld pointers, and US law treats them seriously: FAA civil fines of up to $11,000 per violation, and federal criminal penalties of up to five years and $250,000.225 For shows, the FDA lists "distraction hazards for pilots and drivers" among the risks of a badly run display and points producers to the FAA's outdoor laser material; UK guidance from the Health and Safety Executive says outdoor beams should follow Civil Aviation Authority guidance, with a notice to pilots filed.2830 The detail is in the rules chapter.

Are planetarium laser shows still a thing?#

Yes. At the University of Arizona's Flandrau planetarium, every laser show sold out for 18 months, showings went from two to three every other week, and total attendance reached 46,339 in fiscal 2023, up from about 32,000, with the laser shows named as a significant contributor.92 Fiske Planetarium in Boulder marked its 50th year in 2025 with a laser and music show whose classic rock program sold out before its September 19 debut.109 Saint Louis Science Center lists Nutcracker, Trans-Siberian Orchestra and Taylor Swift laser shows for December 2026 at $14.108 Seattle's Laser Dome is still running after about 50 years, even as its parent institution laid off staff in March 2026.100 The one place they are gone is where they started: Laserium left Griffith Observatory in 2002 and never went back.5 The full story, including who makes the shows and who clears the music, is in the planetarium chapter.

Can you buy a laser show projector?#

The published rules differ by country. What follows describes them and is not advice on any particular purchase.

United States (law, with FDA guidance on top). Laser show projectors are demonstration laser products, and without a variance from the FDA they are limited to Class IIIa: 5 mW of visible output. Class IIIb and Class IV show equipment needs variance approval "before sale or use". The paperwork is a Product Report (Form 3632) from the maker, a Laser Light Show Report (Form 3640) and a Variance Application (Form 3147) from the producer, and the FDA's page is blunt about the order of events: "Your firm may begin to produce laser light shows only after receipt of a variance approval letter."28 The agency's guidance to the trade, Laser Notice 51 of May 27, 2001, adds that "projectors may only be delivered to customers having an approved variance", and that even demonstrating a projector to a customer counts as putting on a show.219

Imports into the US. FDA Import Alert 95-04, first issued in December 1997 and revised on July 31, 2026, lets border staff detain, without examining them, laser pointers, light show projectors and similar products from firms on its list. The grounds include output above 5 mW, missing warning labels and no product report on file. The list runs to roughly 120 firms, nearly all of them in China, and one entry for light show projectors carries the note "Lack of variance".220 The FDA's laser light show page also links a safety communication in which the agency recommends against certain named projector models.28

United Kingdom. Consumer laser products fall under the standard BS EN 50689:2021 and may be Class 1, Class 2 or a subgroup of Class 3R. The UK Health Security Agency's advice, updated on March 18, 2025, says Class 3B and Class 4 lasers "are not suitable for general consumer use", and warns that pointers labeled Class 2 have measured as 3B or 4. Shining a laser at vehicles and aircraft is covered by the Laser Misuse (Vehicles) Act 2018.29 For shows, the HSE's guidance describes no license like the US variance. The duty sits with organizers, venues, suppliers and operators under general safety law, and the HSE's older HS(G)95 booklet has been withdrawn in favor of a free guide from the trade body PLASA, which the HSE endorses.30

European Union. The published picture is patchier. In Germany the OStrV regulation requires a written laser safety officer appointment for Class 3R, 3B or 4 lasers unless the employer has the expertise.18 The 2025 record show in Amsterdam was run to the laser safety standard DIN EN 60825-1, with sign-off by outside testers.255

Is it safe to go to a laser show?#

The FDA's summary of a compliant show is that it will "keep hazardous lasers away from the audience"; the risks it lists for badly handled lasers are eye injury, skin burns, fire and the distraction of pilots and drivers.28 Phones are less protected than eyes. ILDA's line is that "Camera sensors are, in general, more susceptible to damage than the human eye."206 On light and sound, at least one planetarium tells people who are sensitive to either not to attend.108 The rest - classes, exposure limits, injuries on the record, camera damage - is in the safety chapter.

How do you become a laserist?#

There is no single door. A laserist or laser artist performs or operates the show, often mixing pre-programmed cues with live control. Seattle's Laser Dome has six staff laser artists who perform live on Pangolin Beyond.41 One of them, Emily Early-Griffith, describes the job as part preparation, part performance: "We have stuff that we've designed and choreographed beforehand."100

The formal training is short and mostly about safety. ILDA runs a Lasershow Operator course of 4 hours plus a 45-minute test, a Laser Safety Officer course for light shows of about 9 hours including a 2-hour test, and audience-scanning training; other providers run one-day and two-day courses. ILDA's 2025 course list gives no prices.18 The same classes, plus laser content creation, are taught at the LDI trade show in Las Vegas, where the content class earns ETCP renewal credits.304

Whether any of it is required depends on the map. Germany requires a written laser safety officer appointment. Arizona requires each registrant to designate one. The US federal variance form does not mention one except where audience scanning is approved. The UK has no formal qualification.18 ILDA's company accreditation asks for an employee who has passed a Laser Safety Officer course.265 The working day of an operator is in the programming chapter.

What is coming next?#

Less than the hardware might suggest, on the regulatory side.

  • US rules: unchanged where shows are concerned. The FDA re-issued Laser Notice 56 on February 21, 2023. It says products that conform to the international standard IEC 60825-1 Ed. 3 will not face enforcement of the matching parts of 21 CFR 1040.10 and 1040.11 - and it expressly leaves out variances and the rules for demonstration laser products. The show variance system stands as it was.221
  • US paperwork: by email. Variance filings now go to the FDA's device center by email, and the separate filing with Dockets Management is no longer required.28
  • US imports: still watched. Import Alert 95-04 was revised on July 31, 2026.220
  • UK consumer advice: rewritten around EN 50689, in the update published on March 18, 2025.29
  • Safety that acts without the operator. The 2025 record show in Amsterdam ran with automatic beam interruption, and the HSE lists scan-failure safety systems, emergency stops and barriers among the standard controls for a display.25530
  • Brighter single units and wider beams. The 58 W and 60 W projectors at Sphere in 2026 and the variable-divergence units on stadium and festival stages are what the trade press has been writing about since 2023.245244
  • Lasers inside things that are not laser shows. The laser-source searchlight is one example.23 The far larger one is the video projector, which is what the multi-billion-dollar "laser projector" market forecasts describe.267

Quick answers

Do you need a license for a laser light show?
In the US, a show using Class IIIb or IV lasers needs an FDA variance approved before the first show, and the FDA charges no fee for it. UK guidance describes no equivalent license; responsibility sits with organizers, venues, suppliers and operators under general safety law. Requirements for a laser safety officer vary by country and state.
Is it legal to buy a laser show projector?
In the US, show projectors above 5 mW of visible output need an FDA variance before sale or use, and FDA guidance says dealers may deliver them only to customers who hold one. In the UK, official advice says Class 3B and 4 lasers are not suitable for general consumer use. The FDA also keeps an import alert that lets border staff detain projectors from listed firms.
Are laser shows replacing fireworks?
Some cities have made the switch: Mexico City used a laser show for New Year's Eve 2018 over air pollution, and Flagstaff, Arizona did the same for July 4, 2023 over fire risk. Most recent switches have gone to drones instead. Critics, including a Canadian fireworks trade group, say the replacements cost more or lack the feel of fireworks.
Are planetarium laser shows still a thing?
Yes. The University of Arizona's Flandrau planetarium sold out every laser show for 18 months, Fiske Planetarium in Boulder launched a 50th anniversary laser show in 2025, and Seattle's Laser Dome has run for about 50 years. Griffith Observatory, where Laserium began in 1973, has not run laser shows since 2002.
How do you become a laser show operator?
ILDA runs a Lasershow Operator course of 4 hours plus a test and a Laser Safety Officer course of about 9 hours, and classes are taught at the LDI trade show in Las Vegas. Whether a qualification is required depends on the place: Germany and Arizona require a designated laser safety officer, while the UK has no formal qualification.

Glossary

230 terms of art

  • 14 CFR 91.11 The FAA rule against interfering with a crewmember, which the agency uses for civil penalties in laser cases. read more
  • 18 U.S.C. 39A The US federal crime of knowingly aiming a laser pointer at an aircraft or its flight path, punishable by up to five years in prison and a fine. read more
  • 510(k) clearance FDA permission to market a device because it is substantially equivalent to one already on the market. read more
  • Abstracts Non-representational moving laser patterns. read more
  • Adaptive optics A deformable mirror that reshapes itself about 1000 times a second to cancel atmospheric blur. read more
  • Aerial marker Lasers set up to throw beams into the sky as a landmark, as at Coachella's Quasar stage in 2025. read more
  • AIDC Automatic identification and data capture, such as a 2D code. read more
  • Airborne Laser (YAL-1) US Boeing 747-based missile-defense laser test aircraft, tested at Edwards Air Force Base from 2002 and retired to storage in 2012. read more
  • Analog modulation Continuous brightness control of each laser, usually 256 steps per color. read more
  • Anchor point Repeated points at a line end or corner that give the scanners time to stop or turn. read more
  • ANSI Z136.1 The US voluntary consensus standard for safe laser use, which OSHA cites as guidance. read more
  • Argon-ion laser A gas laser with strong blue and green lines, first seen working on February 14, 1964. The bright beam of 1970s rock tours. read more
  • Art-Net A network transport for DMX lighting control data. read more
  • Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors (ABC) A state-law alternative to bankruptcy, used by Glowforge in 2025. read more
  • Assist gas Gas blown down a cutting nozzle: oxygen adds heat by burning the metal, nitrogen only blows out the melt. read more
  • Audience scanning Beams deliberately swept through the crowd at levels meant to stay within the exposure limit. Excluded by the standard conditions of a US variance. read more
  • Automated show A laser show run from recorded control tracks, with nobody playing it. Lovelight (1977) is described as the first. read more
  • Aversion response The blink or turn-away reflex to bright light. Exposure limits for visible beams assume it takes 0.25 seconds. read more
  • Beam Attenuation Map (BAM) A 64 by 64 brightness map over a zone that dims or blocks output in chosen areas (Pangolin's term). read more
  • Beam show A show seen in mid-air: shapes scanned through haze as beams, fans, cones and tunnels. read more
  • Beam table A row of actuators that switch the beam to fixed positions, each aimed at a bounce mirror. read more
  • Beam termination Ending a beam on a surface so it goes no farther into the sky or the crowd. read more
  • BEYOND Pangolin's laser show control software, named in most festival case studies. read more
  • Blanking Switching the beam off while the scanners move between parts of an image. read more
  • Boost phase The early powered flight of a missile, when the Airborne Laser was designed to engage it. read more
  • Bureau of Radiological Health (BRH) The US Food and Drug Administration unit that inspected laser light shows in the late 1970s. read more
  • Busbar A metal link joining battery cells into modules and packs, often laser welded. read more
  • Busking Running cues live to the music instead of playing back a time-coded show. read more
  • CDRH The FDA's Center for Devices and Radiological Health, which receives laser product reports and variance applications. read more
  • Chirped pulse amplification Stretching a light pulse, amplifying it, then compressing it again; the subject of half the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics. read more
  • Class 3B / IIIb Above 5 mW up to 500 mW. Hazardous even for brief accidental direct exposure. read more
  • Class 3R / IIIa Up to 5 mW of visible output; the most a US show projector may emit without an FDA variance. read more
  • Class 4 / IV Above 500 mW. An eye and skin hazard from the direct or reflected beam, and often a fire hazard. read more
  • Class IIIa (3R) Visible output up to 5 mW; the US ceiling for laser pointers and for show projectors without a variance. read more
  • Class IIIb (3B) Output from 5 to 500 mW; in the US it cannot legally be promoted as a laser pointer or demonstration laser. read more
  • Class IV (4) The class above IIIb; US show projectors in this class need an FDA variance. read more
  • Closed loop A scanner whose position detector feeds a servo amplifier that corrects the mirror. read more
  • Coherence Light waves staying in step in phase, because each stimulated photon copies the one that triggered it. read more
  • COIL Chemical oxygen iodine laser, the megawatt-class laser carried by the Airborne Laser. read more
  • Consulting physician (Texas) The doctor a Texas laser hair removal facility must have a written contract with. read more
  • Continuous wave (CW) Laser output that is steady over time rather than pulsed. read more
  • Copper-vapor laser A gas laser used on large 1990s tours; Pink Floyd's 1994 US tour carried two 50 W systems. read more
  • Corner cube retroreflector A three-mirror prism that sends light straight back where it came from; Apollo 11's panel holds 100 of them. read more
  • Cost per shot The operating cost of one laser engagement, the main argument governments make for laser weapons. read more
  • Critical Flight Zone FAA airspace within 10 NM of an airport up to 10,000 ft above ground, capped in Advisory Circular 70-1B at 5 microwatts/cm2. read more
  • DAC Digital-to-analog converter: the hardware that turns software output into the voltages a projector takes. read more
  • Dazzler (optical distracter, ocular interrupter) A visible laser meant to cause temporary, reversible glare, used as a warning at checkpoints and at sea. read more
  • Demarche A formal diplomatic protest. read more
  • Demonstration laser product The US legal term for a laser made, designed, intended or promoted for demonstration, entertainment, advertising display or artistic composition. read more
  • Dichroic A mirror or filter that passes one color and reflects others; used to merge red, green and blue into one beam. read more
  • Diffraction grating A ruled sheet that splits one beam into a fan of many. The Who's 1975 effect used a hand-held one. read more
  • Diffraction limit The smallest spot a lens can focus light into, set by the wavelength. read more
  • Direct diode A semiconductor chip that lases when current is applied to it, with no crystal in between. read more
  • Directed energy weapon A weapon that uses focused energy, such as a laser or microwaves, instead of a projectile. read more
  • Divergence How fast a beam widens as it travels. Doubling it halves the hazard distance. read more
  • DPSS Diode-pumped solid state: an 808 nm diode pumps a crystal lasing at 1064 nm, which a second crystal doubles to 532 nm green. read more
  • DragonFire UK laser weapon led by MBDA UK, due on a Royal Navy Type 45 destroyer by 2027. read more
  • DWPE Detention without physical examination: holding an import at the port because its firm is on an FDA import alert's list. read more
  • EDFA (erbium-doped fiber amplifier) A stretch of fiber laced with erbium and pumped by a laser at 980 or 1480 nm, which boosts 1550 nm signals without turning them into electronics. read more
  • Embedded laser The higher-class laser inside a Class 1 enclosed product; its class applies during service. read more
  • Energy level One of the fixed amounts of energy an atom or molecule can hold; the lowest is the ground state. read more
  • EUV Extreme ultraviolet lithography light at 13.5 nm, made by hitting tin droplets with a laser. read more
  • Excimer laser An ultraviolet gas laser that removes tissue in very thin layers with little heat; the laser behind PRK and LASIK. read more
  • Excimer laser annealing (ELA) Melting and recrystallizing amorphous silicon with an excimer line beam to make low-temperature polysilicon. read more
  • FB4 Pangolin's networked laser control hardware. read more
  • Femtosecond laser A laser whose pulses last quadrillionths of a second, used to cut corneal flaps or lenticules. read more
  • Fenning Award ILDA's award for technical achievement. read more
  • Fiber laser A solid-state laser whose gain medium is a doped optical fiber; it outsold CO2 for cutting from 2015. read more
  • Flame cutting Laser cutting with oxygen assist, which adds heat through an exothermic reaction. read more
  • Flicker Visible strobing when a frame has so many points that it is redrawn too few times a second. read more
  • Fog A thicker, billowing mist that shows swirls inside the beams. read more
  • Form 7140-1 The FAA's Notice of Proposed Outdoor Laser Operation(s), filed at least 30 days before an outdoor show. read more
  • Fractional resurfacing Treating skin in a grid of tiny columns, leaving untreated skin between them to speed healing. read more
  • Frame One complete list of points for one image; frames played in sequence make animation. read more
  • Frye hearing A US court hearing on whether a scientific method is generally accepted before evidence from it is admitted. read more
  • Fusion cutting Cutting by melting the metal and blowing the melt out with inert gas such as nitrogen. read more
  • Gain medium The material where amplification happens: a gas, crystal, dye, fiber or semiconductor. read more
  • Galvo Galvanometer scanner: a limited-rotation motor with a small mirror on its shaft. Two of them aim the beam. read more
  • Geometric correction Warping the output with sliders or a mesh so shapes land correctly on the target. read more

  • HELIOS High-Energy Laser with Integrated Optical Dazzler and Surveillance, a Lockheed Martin system delivered to USS Preble in 2022. read more

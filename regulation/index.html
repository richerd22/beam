Why the FDA regulates lasers

The short version: In the United States a laser is legally an electronic product that gives off radiation, which puts it under the Food and Drug Administration. The FDA's performance standard sorts every laser into a class, says which labels and safety features each class needs, and holds pointers, toys and show projectors to low power unless the agency grants a variance. Enforcement runs mostly through paperwork and ports, while other countries reached pointers by a different road, treating the strong ones as weapons.

Why is the FDA in charge of lasers at all?#

The legal root is the Radiation Control for Health and Safety Act of 1968, Public Law 90-602.444 In 1990 the Safe Medical Devices Act moved that law into Chapter V of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, where it now lives as sections 532, 534 and 542 (21 U.S.C. 360ii, 360kk and 360ss).444

The office that runs it is the FDA's Center for Devices and Radiological Health (CDRH).445 For lasers its work splits three ways: it writes and amends the performance standard, it issues guidance, including a numbered series of Laser Notices, and it acts on products, from repair and refund orders to holding shipments at the border.446447220

That makes it a regulator of products, not people. The FDA does not license laser users. It regulates what a laser is allowed to be when it leaves the factory, and, for shows, what it is allowed to do once someone switches it on. The forms, distances and audience rules for shows are in show laser rules.

What is in the laser performance standard?#

Two sections of federal regulation do most of the work. 21 CFR 1040.10 is the general standard every laser product must meet. 1040.11 adds rules for specific-purpose products, among them medical, surveying and demonstration laser products, the category that holds pointers and light shows.44428

The standard is older than most of the lasers it governs. FDA proposed it in 1974 (39 FR 32097) and published the final rule in 1975 (40 FR 32256), and by the agency's account in 2013 it was last updated in 1985.444

It works in four moves:

  • Classify. Every product is assigned a class by how much laser light it can emit. The classes run from I to IV.
  • Build to the class. Higher classes need more hardware. For a Class IIIb laser FDA lists a warning label, a key switch and a remote interlock connector, plus certification and identification labels and instructions for safe use.446
  • Certify. The manufacturer certifies that the product meets the standard and labels it to say so.446
  • Report. Projector makers file a Product Report (Form FDA 3632), show producers a Laser Light Show Report (Form FDA 3640), and both file annual reports.28
US class (21 CFR 1040.10) International name (IEC 60825-1) Visible output Where it shows up in the rules
IIIa 3R up to 5 mW (400-710 nm) The ceiling for laser pointers and for show projectors without a variance44628
IIIb 3B 5 to 500 mW May not legally be promoted as a pointer or demonstration laser446
IV 4 above Class IIIb Show projectors in this class need a variance28

The lower classes and what each means for an eye are in show safety.

A 2023 change lightened the paperwork for products built around someone else's laser. A final rule published January 20, 2023 and effective February 21, 2023 (Docket FDA-2018-N-3303) added conditions in 21 CFR 1010.2(e) under which a product that incorporates an already-certified laser does not need to be certified or reported again.448

What is a laser variance?#

A variance is FDA approval to depart from the standard, which the agency describes as "an alternate means of providing radiation safety."28 The best-known case is the light show. Show projectors are held to Class IIIa under 21 CFR 1040.11(c), so selling or operating Class IIIb or IV show equipment needs an approved variance, applied for on Form FDA 3147.28

The requirement reaches past the person at the console. FDA's list takes in light show producers, projector manufacturers, dealers and distributors, and users of permanent and temporary installations, and a firm may begin producing shows only after the approval letter arrives. Renewals follow Laser Notice 55.28 For beams going outdoors, the same page points to the FAA's Order 7400.2 on outdoor laser operations.28

The standard bends in other places too. The current rules let CDRH approve an "alternate means" of safety in place of a beam attenuator under 1040.10(f)(6), and the 2013 proposal would have let it approve alternate ways of providing certification and identification under 1010.2(d) and 1010.3(b).444

Why can't a laser pointer be stronger than 5 mW?#

Because a pointer is a demonstration product, and FDA regulation limits those to Class IIIa: 5 mW of visible light between 400 and 710 nm.446 FDA puts it flatly: "Class IIIb lasers cannot legally be promoted as laser pointers or demonstration laser products."446

The consequences FDA lists fall on the supply chain. A manufacturer can be required to repair, replace or refund, and imports can be detained or seized by Customs and Border Protection. Several states also require operators of Class IIIb lasers to register and pay annual fees.446

The agency's public warnings are blunter. "The FDA is aware of many child eye injuries caused when children play with laser pointers," it said in a December 2015 warning reported by Healio.449 A beam at or under 5 mW aimed into the eye can cause temporary flash blindness, FDA said, and one over 5 mW can cause irreversible eye injury. Its tips: don't buy pointers for children, and a pointer shown burning, melting or popping balloons is likely over 5 mW.449

What about toy lasers?#

Toys got their own guidance. In December 2014 CDRH issued the final guidance "Minimizing Risk for Children's Toy Laser Products" (Docket FDA-2012-D-1092), noting that the risk is "a particular concern for lasers intended for entertainment purposes."445

The harder line was in the 2013 proposed rule. It would have restricted lasers made or promoted as children's toys to Class 1 "under any conditions of operation, maintenance, service, or failure", with toys defined by expected use by children under 14.444 Class 1 is the bottom of the international scale, well below the 5 mW pointer ceiling.

How does FDA stop noncompliant lasers?#

Mostly at the dock. Import Alert 95-04 covers pointers, gunsights, laser pens, light show and special-effects projectors, levels, toy guns with lasers, key chains and similar devices.220 FDA's stated reason is that it "continues to encounter violations with foreign manufactured laser products".220

Detention without physical examination (DWPE) means a shipment from a firm on the alert's Red List can be held at the port on the listing alone. The problems FDA catalogs are mostly labels and paperwork: no permanent warning label, output over 5 mW, no certification or identification label, no safe-use instructions, a label and manual that disagree on class or output, and no product report on file.220 Importers can ask to recondition or relabel goods on Form FDA 766. The current version was published September 25, 2026, with guidance revised July 31, 2026.220 The Red List is long, and the alert itself is the place to read it.

Show gear has drawn FDA action too. On November 7, 2017, FDA issued a Safety Communication recommending against using projectors from two brands. It said units made on or after July 2, 2015 had not been sold under an approved variance, and some made from 2008 to July 1, 2015 might lack required features such as a manual reset and remote interlock connector.450 It was a product warning, not a warning letter, and no final resolution has been reported. The rest of that story is in every kind of laser case.

Is the US moving to the international standard?#

Slowly, and through guidance more than rules. The international laser standard is IEC 60825-1, and a product built to it has long faced a second set of requirements to sell in the US.

On June 24, 2013, FDA proposed fixing that (Docket FDA-2011-N-0070, RIN 0910-AF87). The proposal would have incorporated many provisions of IEC 60825-1:2007 and the medical laser standard IEC 60601-2-22:2007 by reference, so one set of rules would cover sales in the US and abroad.444 FDA would have kept its own limit on collateral x-ray emission and renamed the key control a "security master control". It would also have set the Class 1 limit for toys, written a 1976 Department of Defense exemption into the rule, and taken effect two years after a final rule.444

What came in 2023 was guidance, not a rule. Laser Notice 56, dated February 2023, says FDA "does not intend to enforce" the matching requirements of 1040.10 and 1040.11 for products that conform to the IEC 60825-1 Ed. 3 and IEC 60601-2-22 Ed. 3.1 clauses FDA has identified as comparable.447 Some IEC clauses "differ significantly", and those fall outside the notice.447 The same year's records-and-reports rule, above, trimmed paperwork but did not touch harmonization.448

Canada went further. Its 2024 rewrite, SOR/2024-196, replaced older rules for laser scanners and demonstration lasers with one broad standard built on IEC 60825-1 Ed. 3.0 (2014), in force a year after its October 9, 2024 publication.451

How do other countries handle laser pointers?#

Where the US caps pointers through product rules, several countries treat the strong ones as something closer to weapons. The common threshold is 1 mW, a fifth of the US ceiling.

Country Rule Threshold What it does
Australia (federal) Customs (Prohibited Imports) Regulations 1956, from July 1, 2008 above 1 mW Hand-held pointers treated as weapons for import452; the radiation agency calls anything above 1 mW "a prohibited weapon"453
Australia (New South Wales) Permit required from July 18, 2008 above 1 mW Having one in a public place without reasonable excuse is an offense; members of approved astronomy groups exempt454
New Zealand Health (High-power Laser Pointers) Regulations 2013, from March 1, 2014; Customs Import Prohibition Order 2019; Summary Offences Act 1981 above 1 mW Sale only by authorized suppliers to authorized recipients; import restricted; possession in public without reasonable excuse an offense455
Canada (aviation) Transport Canada rules above 1 mW No possession outside a private home in greater Montreal, Toronto or Vancouver, or within 10 km of an airport or certified heliport; work, school and astronomy uses exempt230
Canada (products) Canada Consumer Product Safety Act Class 3B or 4 Hand-held consumer pointers in these classes treated as a danger, so they cannot be sold, imported, advertised or manufactured451
United Kingdom Laser Misuse (Vehicles) Act 2018, in force July 10, 2018 no power limit Offense to shine a laser at a vehicle that is moving or ready to move where it dazzles or distracts, or is likely to, the person in control456

The UK law is about the target rather than the product. It covers trains, planes, cars and boats, extends protection to air traffic controllers, and drops the need to prove intent to endanger; the maximum is five years, an unlimited fine or both.457 Canada's new product standard sets no class cap on pointers at all; its ban sits in the consumer safety law instead.451

Do pointer bans work?#

The evidence is mixed. Australia brought in its import ban after "an increase in the number of incidents" from late 2007, and a 2012 government review found no significant drop in incidents after it took effect.452

Canada's case for its 2024 rules leaned on its own numbers. A 2018 Health Canada survey reported 318 laser eye injuries from 2013 to 2017, rising 34.4% a year, and of 581 hand-held lasers Health Canada tested from 2011 to 2023, 86% were mislabeled, unlabeled or incompletely labeled (official figures).451 The government put the cost to industry at $9 million undiscounted over 10 years (estimate).451

A pointer's label is often the only thing telling a buyer what is inside it, and in Canada's testing the label was usually wrong.

Quick answers

What is the legal power limit for a laser pointer in the US?
FDA regulations cap laser pointers at Class IIIa, 5 mW of visible output (400-710 nm). FDA says Class IIIb lasers cannot legally be promoted as laser pointers.
Why are laser pointers banned in Australia?
From July 1, 2008, hand-held laser pointers above 1 mW were added to Australia's prohibited imports and treated as weapons, after a rise in incidents from late 2007. A 2012 government review found no significant drop in reported incidents afterward.
Do laser show companies need FDA approval?
In the US, selling or operating Class IIIb or IV light show equipment needs an FDA-approved variance, applied for on Form FDA 3147. FDA's list covers show producers, projector makers, dealers and distributors, and users of permanent and temporary installations.
What is an FDA laser variance?
It is FDA approval to depart from the laser standard, which FDA describes as an alternate means of providing radiation safety. It is best known for Class IIIb and IV show projectors, and a firm may begin producing shows only after the approval letter arrives.

Laser show rules by country: who regulates what

The rulebook

16 rules, by place

General information, not safety or legal advice.

RuleStatusWhat it saysAudience scanning
21 CFR 1040.10 and 1040.11(c)
United States (federal) · FDA / CDRH		lawDemonstration laser products must meet Class I, IIa, II or IIIa limits; projectors are limited to 5 mW visible unless the FDA approves a variance under 21 CFR 1010.4. sourceNot addressed in the regulation text; handled through variance conditions
Form FDA 3147 standard variance conditions (items 13c, 13d, 13l)
United States (federal) · FDA / CDRH		conditionVariance conditions, binding on the variance holder rather than a regulation of general application: laser light above Class I limits kept 3.0 m above and 2.5 m lateral to or below where the public may be; operator in direct control; FAA notice for open airspace. sourceNot permitted except diffuse reflections from the atmosphere, added scattering media and target screens; specific approval needed
Laser Notice 55 (2007)
United States (federal) · FDA / CDRH		guidanceEligible Class IIIb/IV show variances roll over one year at a time if the annual report (due September 1) is filed. sourceAudience scanning variances excluded from the streamlined renewal
Advisory Circular 70-1B; Form 7140-1
United States (airspace) · FAA		guidanceNotice of outdoor laser operations requested at least 30 days before the event; flight zone limits of 50 nW/cm2 (Laser Free), 5 microwatts/cm2 (Critical), 100 microwatts/cm2 (Sensitive) and the MPE elsewhere; FAA issues a letter of determination. source
18 U.S.C. 39A
United States (federal) · US Congress / Department of Justice		lawKnowingly aiming a laser pointer beam at an aircraft or its flight path is punishable by fine, up to 5 years, or both. source
12 NYCRR Part 50 (Industrial Code Rule 50)
New York · Department of Labor		lawRegistration of laser installations and mobile lasers; certificate of competence to operate a mobile laser; owners name a laser safety officer. No light-show-specific section. source
25 Texas Administrative Code 289.301
Texas · Department of State Health Services		lawClass 3B and 4 lasers registered; a registration certificate and valid FDA variance before any light show; written notice 7 days before each show. source
32 Ill. Adm. Code 315.140
Illinois · Illinois Emergency Management Agency		lawWritten notice at least 10 working days before a show with FDA variance documentation; nothing above Class 2 less than 3 m above audience standing surfaces or within 2.5 m laterally. sourceAudience locations limited to Class 1; scanning must not exceed Class 1 on the audience even in a scan failure
105 CMR 121
Massachusetts · Department of Public Health Radiation Control Program		lawClass 3B and 4 facilities register and name a laser safety officer; 10 working days' notice for out-of-state lasers; entertainment lasers need an FDA variance and separations of 10 ft above and 8 ft to the side or below under continuous operator control. sourceAudience areas limited to Class 1
General health and safety duties; HS(G)95 withdrawn
United Kingdom · Health and Safety Executive; local authorities		lawNo laser show license. Duty holders must assess and control the risks and use competent contractors; HSE has withdrawn HS(G)95 and points to PLASA's guide. source
Guidance for the Safety of Display Lasers (2019)
United Kingdom · PLASA		industry practiceNot compulsory; PLASA says following it will normally be enough to comply with the law and inspectors may cite it as good practice. source
CAP 736 (2011) and CAA web guidance
United Kingdom (airspace) · Civil Aviation Authority		guidanceNotice of outdoor laser displays: CAP 736 says organizers should give 28 days' advance notification on form DAP 1918; the CAA's newer web page requests a minimum of 7 days. Legal backstop is Air Navigation Order 2016 articles 240, 241 and 265. source
OStrV section 5(2)
Germany · Employer, under federal occupational safety law		lawAn employer who lacks the required expertise personally must appoint a laser safety officer (Laserschutzbeauftragter) with current training in writing before Class 3R, 3B or 4 lasers are operated. source
DGUV Information 203-036 (January 2021)
Germany · DGUV (statutory accident insurers)		guidanceExposure limits must hold in the audience area up to 2.70 m; show area separated by a barrier at least 0.8 m high with 1 m lateral distance; a competent person verifies limits on site. sourceNot banned outright: a beam in the audience space is treated as safe only if attenuated, widened, split or moved fast enough that limits are not exceeded
Canadian Aviation Regulations 601.19-601.21
Canada · Transport Canada		lawWritten permission needed to aim a laser into navigable airspace; possessing a hand-held laser over 1 mW outside a private dwelling is prohibited within 10 km of airports and certified heliports and in the greater Montreal, Toronto and Vancouver regions. source
Audience scanning safety recommendations
International · ILDA		industry practiceRecommended ceilings at the closest audience point of 2.5 mW/cm2 for static beams and 10 mW/cm2 for continuously scanned beams; scan-fail shut-off target of 10 ms, preferably 1 ms. sourceSupported within the MPE; ILDA members affirm that pulsed lasers should never be used

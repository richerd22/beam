Timeline

  1. 1960-05-16First laser works Theodore Maiman and Irnee d'Haenens see laser action from a ruby crystal at Hughes Research Laboratories. source
  2. 1960-12-13First continuous gas laser Javan, Bennett and Herriott run a helium-neon laser at Bell Labs at 1,153 nm infrared. source
  3. 1962Red helium-neon line White and Rigden at Bell Labs get the 632.8 nm red line. source
  4. 1964-02-14Argon-ion laser first seen William Bridges observes argon-ion lasing at Hughes; krypton lines follow that July. These gas lasers make colored shows possible. source
  5. 1964-02-14Argon-ion laser William Bridges sees the 488 nm blue line at Hughes Research Labs. source
  6. 1967Rockne Krebs shows laser beams as sculpture "Sculpture Minus Object" at the Washington Gallery of Modern Art used red beams, fog and mirrors. source
  7. 1967Rockne Krebs shows laser sculpture "Sculpture Minus Object" in Washington uses red beams, fog and mirrors. Other accounts date his first laser work to 1968. source
  8. 1969-05-09Audio/Video/Laser at Mills College Lowell Cross, David Tudor and Carson Jeffries stage what Cross calls the first multi-color laser light show with x-y scanning. source

  10. 1970-03-18Pepsi Pavilion opens at Expo '70 A four-color krypton laser system by Cross and Jeffries runs in Osaka; Cross gives the dedication date as March 15. source
  11. 1972-08-01New York regulates lasers Industrial Code Rule 50 takes effect, ahead of the US federal laser standard. source

  15. 1975-10-18The Who's argon laser at Leicester A 4 W argon laser fanned through a diffraction grating at Granby Halls. source

  17. 1977FDA Laser Notice 22 Light shows fall under the federal standard and show producers count as manufacturers. FDA pages date it November 22 or 23. source

  19. 1977-02-04"Lovelight" runs from stored images Described by LaserFX as the first automated laser show: images drawn on a digitizing pad and stored in PROMs, control tracks on an 8-track recorder. source
  20. 1978"Icarus" on steam screens Laser images projected onto steam screens over Washington DC in summer 1978. source
  21. 1978-07-10Time reports FDA inspectors at light shows The Bureau of Radiological Health had told Blue Oyster Cult to adjust its show and halted a preview on the Mall in Washington. source
  22. 1981Metal-ceramic ion tubes Coherent introduces Innova tubes, lifting tube life from about 1,500 hours toward 5,000. source
  23. 1981-10-21Jarre plays the laser harp in China Five concerts in Beijing and Shanghai, on a harp ordered from Bernard Szajner; 218,000 attended by the artist's figure. source
  24. 1983Stone Mountain laser show opens The Georgia park's mountain-side show begins; the park estimated 20 million viewers in its first 25 years. source
  25. 1983Stone Mountain Lasershow begins A mountain face in Georgia becomes a laser screen about 400 ft square. source
  26. 1985-08-2021 CFR 1040.11(c) takes its current form The demonstration laser rule names Classes I, IIa, II and IIIa (50 FR 33702). source
  27. 1986Pangolin's first product, Laser PC The company's About page gives 1986 for its founding; another company page says 1985. source
  28. 1986-04-05Rendez-vous Houston Jarre uses downtown skyscrapers as screens; attendance is estimated at 1.3 million or 1.5 million. source

  30. 1986-08-16ILDA founded The International Laser Display Association holds its first organizational meeting in Lake Tahoe. source
  31. 1986-08-20US manual-reset rule Class IV laser systems made after this date must need a manual reset after an interlock trip or a power loss over 5 seconds. source

  34. 1992PCAOM and the Cambridge 6800 The polychromatic acousto-optic modulator and Cambridge Technology's 6800 galvo both arrive. source
  35. 1992-06-24ILDA test pattern adopted The circle-in-square pattern becomes the common way to tune and rate scanners. source
  36. 1994Pink Floyd tours with copper-vapor lasers The US tour carried two complete 50 W copper-vapor laser systems built by Oxford Lasers. source
  37. 1995LD2000 and Phoenix Pangolin dates Lasershow Designer 2000 with QM2000 hardware to 1995; development of Phoenix Showcontroller began the same year. source

  39. 1995-10ILDA Test Pattern Rev. 002 The circle-in-a-square tuning frame, at 12,000 or 30,000 points per second. source
  40. 1996HSE publishes HS(G)95 UK guidance on display laser safety, published in October to replace note PM19 of 1980; since withdrawn. source
  41. 1998-12-22O'Hagan thesis A Loughborough University PhD on entertainment laser risk finds audience scanning not acceptable as then practiced. source
  42. 1999Scanners pass 50 kpps A modified Cambridge 6800 shows the test pattern at 52,083 points per second at 6 degrees. source

  46. 2002First OPSL ships Coherent sells a 488 nm optically pumped semiconductor laser as an ion-laser replacement. source

  48. 2004First multicolor laser moving head LOBO dates its ZAPHIR, a scanned laser in a moving-head fixture, to this year. source
  49. 2004Plug-in RGB 5 W full-color DPSS lasers that run from a standard outlet are on offer. source
  50. 2007Roger Waters adds a solid-state laser prism Five direct-diode solid-state lasers totaling over 100 W drew a rotating 3D prism above the stage. source
  51. 2007-09-25FDA Laser Notice 55 Streamlined yearly renewal for eligible show variances; audience scanning excluded. source
  52. 2008QuickShow and FB3QS Pangolin's entry software with a USB DAC (maker's date). source
  53. 2008Aquamarine festival eye injuries Festivalgoers in Kirzhach, Russia, are treated for retinal injuries after a July laser show; reports give 12, 29 or more than 30 patients. source
  54. 2009-01-19Global Rainbow first shown Yvette Mattern's seven parallel laser beams, one per color of the spectrum, debut in New York. source
  55. 2010Blue diodes reach hobbyists 445 nm laser diodes pulled from Casio data projectors were sold on at about $46 each, by one hobbyist's account. source
  56. 2011BEYOND Pangolin's flagship show software (maker's date). source
  57. 2012-02-1418 U.S.C. 39A Aiming a laser pointer at an aircraft becomes a US federal crime under Public Law 112-95. source
  58. 2012-04Tupac "hologram" at Coachella A 2D projection reflected off a slanted transparent film - Pepper's ghost; the vendor's CEO said it was not holographic. source
  59. 2013Green diodes take over Laserworld dates the show industry's switch from DPSS green to direct green diodes to this year. source
  60. 2014FB4 and IDTF Rev. 011 Pangolin's network DAC (maker's date); ILDA's .ild file format reaches Revision 011 on November 16. source
  61. 2014-12-04Seoul club case report Clinical Ophthalmology publishes two retinal injuries after a club laser show; both patients recovered after surgery. source
  62. 2015IDN adopted ILDA members vote in Dubai to adopt the IDN Stream specification for laser data over networks. source
  63. 2017-11-18314-fixture Guinness record ER Productions ran 314 laser fixtures at 1,377 W outside the Las Vegas Convention Center during LDI. source
  64. 2018-08-13Lasers as lighting fixtures PLSN describes the push to run scanned lasers from standard lighting consoles like any other fixture. source
  65. 2018-12-31Mexico City swaps fireworks for lasers New Year's Eve fireworks were replaced with a concert and laser show on Reforma Avenue, for air-pollution reasons. source
  66. 2019-05-24PLASA display laser guidance PLASA publishes the guide HSE now points to in place of HS(G)95. source
  67. 2019-10-17642-beam record Image Engineering set a Guinness record with 642 laser beams at Staples Center, Los Angeles. source
  68. 2020-06-01Canada restricts hand-held lasers SOR/2020-124 is registered, banning hand-held lasers over 1 mW near airports and in three metro regions. source
  69. 2021DGUV Information 203-036 Germany's accident insurers issue the January 2021 edition of their show laser guidance. source
  70. 2021-10-28MadLaser beta garageCube adds laser output to the MadMapper world. source
  71. 2022-02-14FAA Advisory Circular 70-1B Current FAA guidance on outdoor laser operations; Change 1 followed on May 5, 2022. source
  72. 2023150,000 OPSLs Coherent reports more than 150,000 OPSLs in the field. source
  73. 2023-04Stone Mountain's classic show ends The 40-year laser show is replaced by one with lasers, fireworks and drones. source
  74. 2023-07-04Flagstaff replaces July 4 fireworks The Arizona city ran a free laser show set to music, citing extreme fire risk. source
  75. 2023-07-20Laser-source searchlight reported Claypaky's Skylos uses a 300 W white laser source; the maker claims output equal to a 4,000 W xenon lamp. source
  76. 2025-07-191,100-source Guinness record Verknipt ArenA at the Johan Cruijff ArenA, Amsterdam, set the current largest laser show record with 1,100 laser emission sources. source
  77. 2026-02-25FAA reports 2025 laser strikes 10,994 strikes on aircraft in 2025, down 14% from 2024; the record is 13,304 in 2023. source
  78. 2026-07-31FDA revises its laser import alert Import Alert 95-04, first issued in December 1997, covers laser pointers and light show projectors from listed firms. source

Glossary

106 terms of art

  • Abstracts Non-representational moving laser patterns. read more
  • Analog modulation Continuous brightness control of each laser, usually 256 steps per color. read more
  • Anchor point Repeated points at a line end or corner that give the scanners time to stop or turn. read more
  • Argon-ion laser A gas laser with strong blue and green lines, first seen working on February 14, 1964. The bright beam of 1970s rock tours. read more
  • Art-Net A network transport for DMX lighting control data. read more
  • Audience scanning Beams deliberately swept through the crowd at levels meant to stay within the exposure limit. Excluded by the standard conditions of a US variance. read more
  • Automated show A laser show run from recorded control tracks, with nobody playing it. Lovelight (1977) is described as the first. read more
  • Aversion response The blink or turn-away reflex to bright light. Exposure limits for visible beams assume it takes 0.25 seconds. read more
  • Beam Attenuation Map (BAM) A 64 by 64 brightness map over a zone that dims or blocks output in chosen areas (Pangolin's term). read more
  • Beam show A show seen in mid-air: shapes scanned through haze as beams, fans, cones and tunnels. read more
  • Beam table A row of actuators that switch the beam to fixed positions, each aimed at a bounce mirror. read more
  • Beam termination A surface or unit placed to catch a laser beam at the end of its path. read more
  • Blanking Switching the beam off while the scanners move between parts of an image. read more
  • Bureau of Radiological Health (BRH) The US Food and Drug Administration unit that inspected laser light shows in the late 1970s. read more
  • Busking Running cues live to the music instead of playing back a time-coded show. read more
  • CDRH The FDA's Center for Devices and Radiological Health, which receives laser product reports and variance applications. read more
  • Class 3B / IIIb Above 5 mW up to 500 mW. Hazardous even for brief accidental direct exposure. read more
  • Class 3R / IIIa Up to 5 mW of visible output; the most a US show projector may emit without an FDA variance. read more
  • Class 4 / IV Above 500 mW. An eye and skin hazard from the direct or reflected beam, and often a fire hazard. read more
  • Closed loop A scanner whose position detector feeds a servo amplifier that corrects the mirror. read more
  • Coherence Light waves lined up in time and space; the reason a laser beam spreads far less than ordinary light. read more
  • Copper-vapor laser A gas laser used on large 1990s tours; Pink Floyd's 1994 US tour carried two 50 W systems. read more
  • Critical Flight Zone FAA airspace within 10 NM of an airport up to 10,000 ft above ground, capped in Advisory Circular 70-1B at 5 microwatts/cm2. read more
  • DAC Digital-to-analog converter: the hardware that turns software output into the voltages a projector takes. read more
  • Demonstration laser product The US legal term for a laser made, designed, intended or promoted for demonstration, entertainment, advertising display or artistic composition. read more
  • Dichroic A mirror or filter that passes one color and reflects others; used to merge red, green and blue into one beam. read more
  • Diffraction grating A ruled sheet that splits one beam into a fan of many. The Who's 1975 effect used a hand-held one. read more
  • Direct diode A semiconductor chip that lases when current is applied to it, with no crystal in between. read more
  • Divergence How fast a beam widens as it travels. Doubling it halves the hazard distance. read more
  • DPSS Diode-pumped solid state: an 808 nm diode pumps a crystal lasing at 1064 nm, which a second crystal doubles to 532 nm green. read more
  • Fenning Award ILDA's award for technical achievement. read more
  • Flicker Visible strobing when a frame has so many points that it is redrawn too few times a second. read more
  • Fog A thicker, billowing mist that shows swirls inside the beams. read more
  • Frame One complete list of points for one image; frames played in sequence make animation. read more
  • Galvo Galvanometer scanner: a limited-rotation motor with a small mirror on its shaft. Two of them aim the beam. read more
  • Geometric correction Warping the output with sliders or a mesh so shapes land correctly on the target. read more
  • Grand rights Dramatic performance rights - music used to tell a story, as in opera or musical theater. They are negotiated directly with the rights holder and are outside a blanket license. read more
  • Graphics show A show in which the laser draws logos, text and animation on a surface. read more
  • Haze A very fine, evenly spread mist that makes beams visible as smooth cones and sheets. read more
  • Helium-neon laser A low-power gas laser. Its red 632.8 nm line, found in 1962, gave the first continuous visible beam sold commercially. read more
  • Hologram A recording of the full wavefield of light that replays a true 3D image when lit by a laser. read more
  • IAPLC ILDA Accredited Professional Lasershow Company: an industry credential requiring documented shows, client references, a staff member who has passed a Laser Safety Officer course and a signed safety affirmation. read more
  • IDN ILDA Digital Network, a protocol for streaming laser graphics, effects and DMX data over a network. read more
  • IDTF (.ild) ILDA Image Data Transfer Format, for exchanging laser show frames and files between systems. read more
  • ILDA The International Laser Display Association, the laser show trade body. read more
  • ILDA Awards The laser display industry's annual prizes, with artistic categories such as Beams/Atmospherics, Graphics, Planetarium and Live Stage. read more
  • ILDA test pattern A standard frame, including a circle in a square, used to tune scanners and blanking at 12,000 or 30,000 points per second. read more
  • Import alert An FDA instruction that lets border staff detain listed firms' products without examining them; Import Alert 95-04 covers laser pointers and light show projectors. read more
  • Ion laser An argon or krypton gas laser; the professional show source before solid state, water-cooled and about 0.05% efficient. read more
  • Irradiance Power spread over an area, in mW/cm2. It falls with distance as a beam widens. read more
  • ISP-DB25 (ILDA cable) The 25-pin analog connection carrying X, Y, color, shutter and interlock signals to a projector. read more
  • kpps Thousands of points per second, the scanner speed rating. read more
  • Krypton-ion laser A gas laser that can give red, yellow, green and blue at once. Laserium split one krypton beam into four colors with prisms. read more
  • Lase-off Laserists showing off their work to each other. First recorded on August 14, 1986. read more
  • Laser Light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation: a source whose light is essentially one wavelength, with its waves in step, in a narrow beam. read more
  • Laser emission source The unit Guinness counts for its largest laser show record: each point where a beam leaves a device, with a beam split by diffraction counted once. read more
  • Laser Floyd The usual name for a planetarium laser show set to Pink Floyd, most often Dark Side of the Moon or The Wall. Several companies have made their own. read more
  • Laser Free Zone FAA airspace closest to runways, where visible laser light is capped at 50 nW/cm2. read more
  • Laser harp An instrument made of upright laser beams. Breaking a beam with the hand triggers a note. read more
  • Laser mapping Laser outlines fitted to the features of a building or 3D object. read more
  • Laser Notice A numbered FDA guidance letter to the laser industry, such as Notice 51 on projector sales or Notice 55 on variance renewal. read more
  • Laser Safety Officer (LSO) The named person responsible for laser safety at a facility or show. Required by name in several US states and in Germany. read more
  • Laser-phosphor projector A video projector in which blue laser diodes light a spinning phosphor wheel and an imaging chip forms the picture; no beam is scanned. read more
  • Laserist The person who performs or operates a laser show; in the analog era, the player of the console. read more
  • Laserium The laser show Ivan Dryer opened at Griffith Observatory in Los Angeles on November 19, 1973, played live to recorded music; it ran there until January 2002. read more
  • Laserium Light Synthesizer (LLS) Laserium's analog performance console, with more than 150 controls for image size, motor speeds, brightness, shape and color, by one former laserist's count. read more
  • Laserock Laserium's first all-rock program, from 1977, and the start of the rock-album planetarium format. read more
  • Laserschutzbeauftragter Germany's laser safety officer, appointed in writing before Class 3R, 3B or 4 lasers are operated by an employer who lacks the required expertise personally. read more
  • Letter of determination The FAA's reply to a notice of an outdoor laser operation, stating an objection or no objection. read more
  • Lidar A scanning laser used to measure distance, as on some cars. read more
  • Liquid sky Haze or fog visibly swirling inside a flat sheet of scanned laser light. read more
  • Lumia A soft, gauze-like moving pattern made by sending a beam through rippled glass or plastic. read more
  • Masking plate Metal plates at the aperture that cut off any output below or outside a set line, whatever the software sends. read more
  • Maximum Permissible Exposure (MPE) The highest level of laser light a standard treats as acceptable for an eye or skin over a given time. For a visible beam and a quarter second it is about 2.5 mW/cm2. read more
  • Nominal Ocular Hazard Distance (NOHD) The distance at which a direct beam has spread enough for its irradiance to fall to the MPE. read more
  • NOTAM A notice to aircrews. The UK CAA normally issues one for a notified outdoor laser display. read more
  • OPSL Optically pumped semiconductor laser: diodes pump a thin gain chip in a small cavity, giving a clean, narrow beam and chosen colors such as 577 nm yellow. read more
  • PCAOM Polychromatic acousto-optic modulator: a tellurium dioxide crystal driven by radio-frequency signals that set color and brightness on gas lasers. read more
  • Pepper's ghost A reflection illusion using a slanted transparent sheet; the method behind stage "holograms". read more
  • Performing rights organization (PRO) A body such as ASCAP, BMI, SESAC or Global Music Rights that licenses the public performance of songs to the business where the music is played. read more
  • Point The smallest thing laser software controls: an X position, a Y position, on or off, and usually a color. read more
  • Point (sample) One X-Y-color position sent to the projector. A frame is a list of them. read more
  • Population inversion The condition lasing needs: more atoms in the excited state than in the ground state. read more
  • Projection zone A named output area with its own size, position, geometric correction and safety settings. read more
  • Raster An image built from closely spaced horizontal lines, as on a TV; too fast for scanner mirrors beyond low resolutions. read more
  • Real-time performance console A generally analog console that lets the laserist change size, position, color and effects live. read more
  • Remote interlock connector The connector US law requires on Class IIIb and IV laser systems; an emergency stop plugs into it. read more
  • Ruby laser The first laser (1960): a pulsed red source at 694 nm. It flashes, so it was no use for drawing. read more
  • Safety observer A person watching the airspace or beam paths who can terminate the beam immediately; ILDA's draft standard calls the role a laser spotter. read more
  • Scan-fail A safeguard that blanks the beam if the scanners stop moving it. read more
  • Scan-failure safety system A system that cuts the beam if the scanning mirrors stop moving; listed by the UK HSE among standard controls for laser displays. read more
  • Scanning safeguard A feature that prevents exposure above the limits if the scanning mirrors slow or stop. Required on scanned laser products in the US by 21 CFR 1040.10(f)(9). read more
  • Show-day rate What a laser company charges per day of show, worked out from equipment write-off, overhead and margin. read more
  • Shutter A mechanical flag inside the projector that blocks the beam ahead of the scanners; the beam attenuator of the US rule. read more
  • Star operator The second person at a Laserium show, who followed a written script to time the house lights and the planetarium's star projector. read more
  • Stimulated emission A passing photon makes an excited atom release a second photon matching it in wavelength, direction and phase. read more
  • Teach track A track on Laserium's four-track show tape that helped new laserists learn the timing. read more
  • Timecode A running clock signal, such as SMPTE or MIDI timecode, that keeps lasers, lights, video and audio on one timeline. read more
  • TTL On/off-only laser control, giving seven colors and no fades. read more
  • Variable divergence A projector feature that lets the operator widen or narrow the beam during a show. read more
  • Variance FDA permission to vary from a requirement of a performance standard - for shows, the 5 mW cap in 21 CFR 1040.11(c) - on stated safety conditions. read more
  • Variance condition A requirement the FDA attaches to a variance approval, binding on the holder. The 3.0 m and 2.5 m audience separations are conditions. read more
  • Vector (in show software) A point with automatic sample placement: the software adds samples along long lines and at corners. read more
  • Vector graphics Images stored as points joined by lines and drawn as outlines. read more
  • White-light laser A mixed argon/krypton ion laser that emits red, green and blue lines from one tube. read more
  • X-Y scanning Drawing with one beam by steering it horizontally and vertically with two mirrors, the way an oscilloscope draws. read more

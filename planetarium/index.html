The planetarium laser show: Laserium, Laser Floyd and why it never died

The short version: The planetarium laser show began on November 19, 1973, when Ivan Dryer's Laserium opened at Griffith Observatory in Los Angeles with one krypton laser and a person playing it live to recorded music. It spread to dozens of cities, turned into the rock-album format of Laser Floyd and Laser Zeppelin, lost its home dome in 2002 and kept going anyway. The shows running now are mostly supplied by a few companies, the projectors are digital, and the album might be Taylor Swift's, but the idea is the one Dryer sold in 1973.

Who invented the planetarium laser show?#

A filmmaker who gave up on film. Ivan Dryer had worked as a guide and lecturer at Griffith Observatory from 1956 to 1968, by his own account, and then made a film of laser light called "Laserimage".51 The film is dated 1970 in some accounts and 1972 in another, and it was preserved in 2018.677935 What it taught Dryer was that 16 mm film captured the colors of laser light poorly, so the light would have to be shown live.45

In the fall of 1970 Dryer, Dale Pelton and Elsa Garmire, a Caltech physicist who had begun making laser images as photographs, demonstrated a helium-neon laser and a lumia wheel in the Griffith dome. The observatory declined.345135 Their company, Laser Images Inc., dates from February 1971 by one account and 1973 by another, with Garmire as its first president.3479 Dryer paid $10,000 for a laser in those years.45

The break came in June 1973, at a demonstration in Garmire's Caltech lab with a krypton laser on loan from Spectra-Physics. More than 100 people were invited and two came. One was William Kaufmann, Griffith's new director.5134

What happened at Griffith Observatory on November 19, 1973?#

Laserium opened on a Monday night, in the hours after the planetarium's regular shows, with Laser Images renting the theater as an outside party.4380 The first show opened with "The Blue Danube", a choice Dryer credited to Stanley Kubrick's "2001".4751

Nearly everything else about the trial comes in several versions. It was four Monday nights in Dryer's book preview and in Fortune, a "four-day trial run" in Dryer's words to LA Weekly, a four-week engagement to the historian Patrick McCray and a one-month test in Dryer's obituary.5147803467 The first house was half full, or mostly full, or about 700 people in a dome that seats 600.478034 The accounts agree on the ending: by the last night about 500 people were being turned away.6751

"It was supposed to be temporary and wound up as a career," Dryer said in 2006.80

How did a Laserium show work?#

One laser, one player, one dome. The projector was built around a 1 W krypton gas laser whose beam was split by prisms into red, yellow, green and blue. The whole unit was roughly 2 x 6 x 3 feet, and Dryer and two colleagues patented it in 1977.34 By one former laserist's account, each laser cost about $30,000 in the 1970s and wanted three-phase 240 V power at 100 A plus cooling water.81

The parts were improvised. Early scanners were meter movements with mirrors glued on, since commercial laser scanners were not yet available. The drifting clouds came from lumia wheels, and the look goes back to Garmire dripping Duco cement onto glass slides, where it hardened into a bubbled surface that scattered the beam.5145 The cooling water had its own failure mode. When toilets flushed in the building, water pressure dropped and the laser could shut off mid-show, which the crew called "the john effect" until a separate water line went in.51

The operator sat at a console of switches and joysticks, the Laserium Light Synthesizer, with more than 150 controls for image size, motor speeds, brightness, shape and color.3481 A four-track tape carried the stereo music plus an introduction and narration, and a "teach track" that helped new laserists learn the timing.34 Every show began with the planetarium's star field, run by a second person, the star operator, who followed a written script to time the house lights and the Zeiss star projector.5181

Was Laserium live or pre-recorded?#

Both, and how much of each depends on who is telling it. Dryer chose the music first and set images to it, then each laserist interpreted that choreography, in his words, "somewhat like a jazz improvisation".51 After the first few years, shows were choreographed and the music edited at company headquarters in Van Nuys, California, and sent out as a master program that the laserist could bend to the crowd.4582

Glenn Thomas, a former laserist, has written the most detailed account, and it is one person's memoir. In his telling the music and a data track synced to it handled some image types, beam on/off and sizing, and he performed the rest, which he puts at 90% of each show. The console, he writes, was "designed to be played LIVE, in real time, by a musician like myself."81 His night at Griffith meant arriving two hours early, running three shows of about 45 minutes at 6:00, 9:15 and 10:30, and swapping about 25 effects between them. Scan glasses stuck, joystick wires came off and the data dropped out, and he fixed it or improvised around it. If the laser would not restart, the house lights came up and the tickets were refunded.81

The freedom was earned. A chief laserist who started in 1986 learned under Dryer and had to win approval before playing for the public, and newcomers followed the choreography before they got room to improvise.83 "You can't jam all the time," Danny Neilson told MEL Magazine in 2016.83 Dryer reckoned about 30% of the audience were repeat visitors.51 The digital descendants of that console are in the programming chapter.

How many cities had Laserium?#

More than anyone has counted the same way twice. Denver's Gates Planetarium took Laserium in August 1974, New York's Hayden in October, San Francisco's Morrison in November and San Diego in January 1975. St. Louis, Miami, Toronto, Kyoto and London followed in 1975 and 1976, and Dryer said London drew 6,800 patrons a week for three months.51 Seattle's dome joined in August 1975 by the venue's account, or in 1976 by Seattle Weekly's.8453

What was the first automated laser show?#

"Lovelight", on February 4, 1977, at the Hayden Planetarium in Boston, produced by a company called Interscan.39 Nobody played it. Its images were drawn on a digitizing pad and stored in PROM chips, and its control tracks were recorded on an 8-track instrumentation recorder alongside the music.39 Tape did the same job elsewhere. Laser Fantasy's first show, "Laser Odyssey", ran from an 8-track with two tracks of music and six of scanner signals, through a modified audio mixer.86

Where did Laser Floyd and Laser Zeppelin come from?#

From the audience. Laserium's first program mixed classical pieces with Pink Floyd, Emerson, Lake and Palmer and Wendy Carlos, and Pink Floyd was a favorite from the first run.7934 Laserock, the first all-rock program, followed in 1977, and "Dark Side of the Moon" in 1984 became what Dryer called the most popular show.51 Seattle's dome began a late-night "The Wall" around 1982, and Rush's "Signals" that year has been called the first album to get the full laser-show treatment.53 ILDA's Patrick Murphy on why Pink Floyd never leaves the schedule: "That's a perennial because of the spacey music and lyrics."53

The titles most people remember came from Laserium's competitors, who supplied shows to domes.

How is the music in a laser show licensed?#

The clearest public statement comes from a producer, and it hands the job to the buyer. Nashville's Adventure Science Center tells domes that take its shows: "It is your institution's responsibility to acquire music performance rights with all applicable licensing agencies." It adds that performance rights for Prince's music are held solely by Global Music Rights.91 Seattle's Pacific Science Center has said it pays licensing fees for its public laser shows, without saying which.41

In the US the agencies in question are the performing rights organizations - ASCAP, BMI, SESAC and Global Music Rights - which license the public performance of songs to the business where the music is played.93 Their blanket licenses cover non-dramatic performance only. Music used to tell a story, as in opera or musical theater, falls under "grand rights" negotiated directly with the rights holder.9495 For scale, ASCAP's 2026 concert schedule charges a percentage of revenue, from 0.80% for rooms of up to 2,500 seats down to 0.10% above 25,000, with a minimum annual fee of $319. That schedule is written for concerts.96

Past that, the public record is thin. Nobody has published a rate schedule for laser shows or planetariums. Nothing in print settles whether a full-album laser show counts as a non-dramatic performance, whether anyone seeks permission to choreograph to a specific recording, or whether Pink Floyd or Led Zeppelin ever formally approved the titles that carry their names. No legal dispute between a band or publisher and a planetarium show producer is on record either. Where an artist has clearly signed off, it tends to be an event: Seattle's dome has hosted album release nights for Radiohead's "Kid A" and Sleater-Kinney's "The Center Won't Hold".84

Why did Laserium leave Griffith Observatory?#

The building closed, and the show was not asked back. New York's Hayden had dropped Laserium in 1982, San Diego went laser-free in the mid-1990s and San Francisco's Morrison held on until 2001. "The peak was 1978, and it was a slow attrition from there," Dryer told Fortune.47 By his account the company spent 1985 to 1997 in bankruptcy proceedings.51

The last Griffith show, "Laser Visions", opened with "The Blue Danube" and was played by laserist Tim Barrett to more than 600 people. About 3,000 tried to get into the final two.51 ILDA's newsletter and Dryer's obituary date it Saturday, January 5, 2002. The observatory's own timeline says Laserium ran until January 6, the day the building closed for renovation.976743 The observatory reopened on November 2, 2006, after a $93 million rebuild, without it.43 Director E.C. Krupp told NPR: "There was not a public outcry: Oh no, you're not running Laserium."85

Laserium tried other rooms: a planned Hollywood "Cyberdome", a 2009 run at the Vine Theatre in Hollywood at $12 or $18 a ticket, and weekend shows at its Van Nuys studio at $14 in 2015.47985 Dryer handed the business to Jon Robertson in December 2009, or sold it, depending on the account.5145 The last ongoing Laserium show, "Aurora Dreams", ran in 2016.79 Dryer died on July 27, 2017, aged 78.67 ILDA had given him its first Career Achievement Award in 1989 and marks his birthday, March 7, as International Laserist Day.38

Why did laser shows survive fulldome video?#

Nobody has tested an answer. What is on record is the demand. At Flandrau in Tucson every laser show sold out for 18 months, showings went from two to three every other week, and total attendance rose from about 32,000 to 46,339 in fiscal 2023, with the university naming laser shows as a significant contributor.92 "It's always been a quintessential idea that planetariums have laser light shows," Flandrau's Shiloe Fontes said.92 Seattle's Laser Dome drew more than 100,000 visitors in 2019, three years after replacing its 1970s krypton system with 10 digital projectors.41

The catalogs have kept up. Rowan University's planetarium lists 43 laser titles, with Beyonce, Daft Punk and The Weeknd alongside Laser Zeppelin and two Laser Floyds.99 And some domes still play it live. In 2020 Seattle had six staff laser artists performing live on current software, working from material that laser artist Emily Early-Griffith described in 2026 as "designed and choreographed beforehand."41100

Denver shows the whole arc. The Gates Planetarium ended laser shows around 1998, with occupancy near 40%, and Laser Fantasy's Stefanie Hare said, "We did not meet the mission of the institution."101 The same dome later ran Laser Taylor Swift, with Laser Fantasy, and extended it.102

What was Phoebe Bridgers' "Lost Weekend" planetarium run?#

An album preview in the format's own house. From August 10 to 14, 2026, Phoebe Bridgers previewed her third album, "Lost Weekend", in planetariums ahead of its August 14 release on Dead Oceans. Each session paired the record with one of two visuals: a fulldome show with photography by Babak Tafreshi, or a laser show designed by Laser Fantasy. Phones and cameras were not allowed.103

Billboard counted 44 planetariums, among them Bozeman, Vancouver, Glasgow and Auckland, in partnership with Spotify. JamBase said "more than 40".104105 On August 13 Bridgers appeared at Liberty Science Center's Jennifer Chalsty Planetarium in Jersey City and performed three songs.104 In Seattle the "Lost Weekend Laser Dome Experience" ran August 12 to 14 at 7:15 and 9 p.m., about an hour each, all sold out, with phones in Yondr pouches and a blunt line on the venue's page: "This is not a performance".106 How many of the 44 rooms ran the lasers and how many the fulldome version has not been published.

Quick answers

What was Laserium and is it still running?
Laserium was Ivan Dryer's laser show, which opened at Griffith Observatory in Los Angeles on November 19, 1973 and ran there until January 2002. It later played other rooms, and the last ongoing Laserium show ran in 2016. Griffith Observatory no longer runs laser shows.
Are planetarium laser shows live or pre-recorded?
Laserium shows were played live by a laserist at an analog console, over recorded music and a data track that handled some effects; one former laserist estimates 90% was performed live. The first fully automated show dates from 1977. Today some domes, including Seattle's Laser Dome, still use live operators.
Who makes Laser Floyd and Laser Zeppelin shows?
Several companies have. Laser Fantasy made "Laser Zeppelin" and "Laser Floyd" in Seattle, Audio Visual Imagineering supplies a library that includes Laser Zeppelin and Pink Floyd titles, and Laserium ran its own Dark Side of the Moon from 1984. Some planetariums, such as Nashville's Sudekum, produce and sell their own.
Do planetarium laser shows pay for the music?
A show producer's published terms put the job on the venue, which must get music performance rights from the licensing agencies, and at least one venue has said it pays licensing fees. Beyond that the public record is thin: no laser-show rate schedule or band authorization for the classic titles has been published.

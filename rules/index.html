The rules: who regulates laser shows, country by country

The short version: In the United States a show laser above 5 mW of visible output is lawful only under a variance from the FDA, and a beam that leaves for open sky brings in the FAA. Most other countries have no laser show license at all: general workplace safety law and the same exposure limit apply, and guidance documents carry the practical detail. Past the US, UK, Germany, Canada and Australia, the country-by-country picture comes from a list the trade association itself calls unofficial.

Who regulates laser shows in the United States?#

The Food and Drug Administration, through its Center for Devices and Radiological Health (CDRH). The authority is the Federal Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act, Chapter V, Subchapter C (Electronic Product Radiation Control), with records and reporting rules in 21 CFR Parts 1000 to 1005 and the performance standards in Parts 1010, 1040.10 and 1040.11.28

Three pieces of that are law and do most of the work:

  • 21 CFR 1040.10(b)(13) defines a demonstration laser product as one made, designed, intended or promoted for demonstration, entertainment, advertising display or artistic composition.6
  • 21 CFR 1040.11(c) says such products must meet the requirements for Class I, IIa, II or IIIa, and "shall not permit human access to laser radiation in excess of the accessible emission limits of Class I" and, where applicable, IIa, II or IIIa. The paragraph's source note cites 50 FR 33702, August 20, 1985.216
  • The effect, in the FDA's own summary: projectors are limited to 5 mW of visible output unless the FDA approves a variance, and Class IIIb and IV light show equipment needs one.28

One piece of guidance set the shape of the industry. Laser Notice 22, dated November 22 or 23, 1977 depending on which FDA page is read, said light shows fall under the standard and that assembling a show from an existing laser makes a "new" product - so the show producer is a manufacturer.28217

New York got there first. Its Industrial Code Rule 50 on lasers took effect on August 1, 1972.209

What is an FDA laser variance, and how is one applied for?#

A variance is, in the FDA's words, "permission to vary from one or more requirements of a performance standard", requested under 21 CFR 1010.4. For shows the agency calls it "an alternate means of providing radiation safety".21828

There are three forms: the Product Report (Form FDA 3632), which describes the projector; the Laser Light Show Report (Form FDA 3640), which describes the show; and the Variance Application (Form FDA 3147).28 A show producer files the application and the show report. A projector manufacturer files all three. A buyer of an already-certified projector needs only the show report and the application.28 Since September 1, 2020 the package goes to CDRH by email, with no separate copy to Dockets Management.218 Production or shows may begin only after the approval letter arrives, and the letter sets the conditions.28

The form in its 04/24 revision says the FDA generally approves for two years.197 Annual reports under 21 CFR 1002.13 are due September 1 and cover July 1 to June 30.198 Under Laser Notice 55 (2007, guidance), eligible Class IIIb and IV show variances roll over a year at a time, to December 31, as long as that year's report is filed; miss it and the variance ends December 31. Audience scanning, invisible-laser effects and reduced clearance distances are excluded from that streamlined path.198

Selling is covered too. Laser Notice 51 (2001, guidance) says manufacturers may deliver projectors only to customers holding an approved variance, that dealers and distributors need their own, and that a dealer's demonstration counts as producing a light show.219 At the border, Import Alert 95-04, first issued in December 1997 and revised July 31, 2026, lets staff detain without physical examination laser pointers, light show projectors and similar products from listed firms. Other covered products can be detained when an examination shows, among other things, output above 5 mW, a missing warning label or no product report.220

International conformance does not open a side door. Laser Notice 56, issued May 8, 2019 and reissued February 21, 2023 (the FDA's index page dates it January 19, 2018), says the agency does not intend to enforce parts of 1040.10 and 1040.11 for products that conform to the comparable clauses of IEC 60825-1 Edition 3. Its section III.B lists 1040.11(c) and 1010.4 as not covered, so the Class IIIa demonstration limit and the variance requirement stand.221217

The system is old. In 2006 ILDA's then executive director wrote in Laser Focus World that the display rules "date back almost 30 years" and had "remained virtually unchanged".222

How high do laser beams have to be above the audience?#

In the US, 3.0 meters. That figure is a condition of the variance, not a line in the regulation: it is printed on Form FDA 3147 among the protections an applicant commits to, and it binds the holder. Item 13d says laser radiation above Class I limits is not permitted at any point less than 3.0 meters above any surface where people other than operators, performers or employees may stand, or within 2.5 meters below or laterally from where such persons may be.197

Other standard conditions on the same form:197

  • Item 13c: no scanning, projection or reflection into audiences or accessible uncontrolled areas "except for diffuse reflections produced by the atmosphere", added scattering media and target screens.
  • Trained operators must directly control the show, be placed where every beam path can be seen, and stop it at once if anything is unsafe; output must not exceed what the effects need.
  • Written setup, alignment and test procedures, and records under 21 CFR 1002.30 and 1002.31.
  • Marked and controlled laser areas, to ANSI Z136.1 or equivalent.
  • Scanning safeguards wherever scanning is relied on to meet the limits.
  • Projectors may be passed only to someone who holds a variance.
  • Operators, performers and employees may not be required or allowed to view radiation above Class I limits.
  • Item 13l: notice to the FAA for projections into open airspace "at any time", setup, alignment and rehearsal included.

What does the FAA have to do with a laser show?#

The FAA regulates navigable airspace under 49 U.S.C. 40103, and it does not permit or license shows. It reviews a notice and issues a letter of determination that says either "FAA has no objection" or that it objects.215 The force comes from the FDA side, where FAA notification is a variance condition.197 The FAA's own document, Advisory Circular 70-1B, "Outdoor Laser Operations" (February 14, 2022, with a change dated May 5, 2022), is guidance.215

The circular applies to anyone planning outdoor visible beams (400 to 700 nm) above 50 nW/cm2 in navigable airspace, or any beam above the MPE there. The filing is FAA Form 7140-1, "Notice of Proposed Outdoor Laser Operation(s)", with a Laser Configuration Worksheet for each configuration, sent to the service center for the area "at least 30 days before the planned event".215 ILDA's one-page overview tells members to submit at least 90 days ahead and says review may take 30 to 90 days; that is trade advice, and the FAA's figure is 30.178

Airspace near airports is divided into zones with brightness caps:

Zone Limit (AC 70-1B, 2022) Extent (FAA Order JO 7400.2R)
Laser Free Zone 50 nW/cm2 or less Within 2 NM of the runway centerline up to 2,000 ft above ground, plus a 3 NM extension 2,500 ft each side of the extended centerline
Critical Flight Zone 5 microwatts/cm2 or less - a level that should not cause veiling glare 10 NM radius from the airport reference point, up to 10,000 ft above ground
Sensitive Flight Zone 100 microwatts/cm2 or less Locally designated; no fixed size
Normal Flight Zone The MPE Everything else

The limits are from the circular's Appendix A and Appendix F; the dimensions are from paragraph 29-1-5 of the air traffic order.215190

The units are easy to misread. The Critical and Sensitive limits are in microwatts per square centimeter. The Advisory Circular spells the unit out - "100 microwatts per square centimeter" for the Sensitive zone - and its glossary gives 5 and 100 with the microwatt symbol. The FAA's web text of Order JO 7400.2R, paragraph 29-1-5, prints the same two limits as "5mW/cm2" and "100mW/cm2", a thousand times larger, which would put both caps above the 2.6 mW/cm2 exposure limit given in the same list. The table uses the circular's microwatts.215190

The circular also describes safety observers - people who can terminate the beam immediately - and allows an electronic protection system in their place if the proponent documents that it meets the performance criteria in SAE AS6029. It says the FAA's interest "does not supersede or invalidate any existing rules or ordinances" of other governments.215

Is it illegal to point a laser at an aircraft?#

In the US it is a federal crime. Under 18 U.S.C. 39A, added by Public Law 112-95 on February 14, 2012, knowingly aiming a laser pointer beam at an aircraft, or at its flight path, is punishable by a fine, up to five years in prison, or both. The exceptions are FAA-authorized research and flight test, Defense and Homeland Security activity, and emergency laser signaling devices.223 The FAA gives the figures as criminal penalties of up to five years and $250,000, and civil fines of up to $11,000 per violation.224

Who regulates laser shows in the UK?#

Nobody issues a laser show license. HSE's position is that duty holders must assess and control the risks and use competent contractors, under general health and safety law; local authorities enforce that law in entertainment premises and handle entertainment licensing.30196

The dedicated guidance has been withdrawn. HS(G)95, "The radiation safety of lasers used for display purposes", was published by HSE in October 1996 to replace a 1980 guidance note, PM19.191 HSE now says HS(G)95 "has been withdrawn" and points to the trade association PLASA's "Guidance for the Safety of Display Lasers".30 PLASA describes that guide as prepared with trade bodies, laser display specialists, venues, local authority environmental health officers, insurers, HSE and Public Health England. It is not compulsory, PLASA says, but following it will normally be enough to comply with the law, and inspectors may cite it as good practice.228 That makes it industry guidance with the regulator's backing.

Consumer sales are a separate matter: the UK Health Security Agency says consumer laser products fall under BS EN 50689:2021, and reports the standard as saying Class 3B and Class 4 lasers "are not suitable for general use by consumers".29

For outdoor beams, the Civil Aviation Authority has two documents that disagree on timing. CAP 736, version 3 of February 1, 2011 and still listed as current, says organizers should give 28 days' advance notification on form DAP 1918 - the sample form in its annex says that notice "MUST be given" - and says the UK "does not prescribe precise dimensions" for beam zones.229 The CAA's current web page says "A minimum of 7 days' notice is requested", that it normally issues a NOTAM and does not charge, and that the legal backstop is articles 240, 241 and 265 of the Air Navigation Order 2016.227 The web page cites the 2016 Order, so it is the newer of the two.

How does Germany regulate laser shows?#

Through occupational safety law, with an officer at the center. Section 5(2) of the OStrV, the workplace ordinance on artificial optical radiation, requires an employer who lacks the required expertise personally to appoint a Laserschutzbeauftragter in writing before operating Class 3R, 3B or 4 lasers. The officer's qualification is shown by passing a course and kept current through further training. The officer supports the risk assessment and the protective measures and monitors safe operation.213

The show-specific detail is in guidance from the statutory accident insurers: DGUV Information 203-036, on laser equipment for show and projection use, January 2021. It defines the audience area as wherever the legal exposure limit values are not exceeded for the whole show, and asks that the limits hold up to 2.70 meters where people can be. The show area is separated by a stage, pit or barrier at least 0.8 meters high with at least 1 meter of lateral distance, and a competent person inspects before first use and after each rebuild and verifies the limits on site for every planned effect.199 It also warns that lasers classified under the 2015 edition of EN 60825-1 can exceed the exposure limits even in Class 1.199

What are the rules in Canada?#

Health Canada's guidance covers laser products, not shows as such. The Canada Consumer Product Safety Act is used against Class 3B and 4 handheld portable lasers; the national standard CAN/CSA E60825-1:15 is identical to IEC 60825-1 (2014) apart from added electrical code requirements; and meeting the US rules in 21 CFR 1040.10 and 1040.11 does not automatically meet Canadian requirements. Installation, operation and training rules sit with the provinces and territories.193

The sky is federal law. Aiming a laser into navigable airspace needs written permission from Transport Canada, sought through a "Notice of proposal to conduct outdoor laser operation(s)"; the prohibition on projecting a bright light source into navigable airspace without authorization is in Canadian Aviation Regulations 601.20.230226 Since 2020, possessing a hand-held laser over 1 mW without a legitimate reason is prohibited outside a private dwelling in the greater Montreal, Toronto and Vancouver regions and within 10 km of any airport or certified heliport, with fines of $1,000 on the spot and up to $5,000 for an individual or $25,000 for a corporation.230226

What about Australia, Japan and everywhere else?#

Australia's aviation regulator, CASA, says organizers "must ensure that the lasers and lights don't pose an aviation safety risk" and points them to its Advisory Circular AC 139-23 and to state and local approvals.231 In South Australia, SafeWork SA issued a safety alert on April 5, 2024 citing growing concern about unsafe use of Class 3B and 4 lasers at entertainment venues. It cites regulation 223(5) of the Work Health and Safety Regulations 2012 on operator training, and says lasers must not be directed where the beam could reach a person, a reflective surface or anything ignitable.232

Japan's JIS C 6802 is the counterpart of IEC 60825-1, and its consumer product safety law covers portable lasers such as pointers; neither is written for professional show systems.192233

For most of the rest of the world, the only compiled reference is ILDA's "Worldwide audience scanning laws" page, which says of itself that it is "NOT official and is not regularly updated". Its main table dates from mid-2014, with some entries revised since.200 What follows is ILDA's account, not the regulators'. Switzerland: since December 2020 only holders of a Swiss certificate may run show lasers, with a higher qualification for audience scanning, and events are reported to the federal health office 14 days ahead. Sweden: a permit from the radiation authority, with scanning declared alongside a risk assessment and on-site measurement. Finland: a permit, and rules that prohibit audience scanning apart from some special permits. Austria: a permit. Netherlands: no show-laser law beyond one on pointers, with practitioners informally keeping the lowest beams at least 3 meters up. France: some regions reported to prohibit Class 4 show lasers. Japan: listed among countries where a permit is required "but this law is often not followed or enforced".200

What is law, what is guidance, and what is industry practice?#

Law is the short list. In the US: the class limits and hardware rules of 21 CFR 1040.10, the 5 mW demonstration cap of 1040.11(c), the state regulations, and 18 U.S.C. 39A.6216223 In Germany, the OStrV and its laser safety officer.213 In Canada, the aviation regulations.230 In the UK, general duties and the Air Navigation Order, with nothing written for shows.30227

Conditions sit one step down and bind only the holder: the 3.0 and 2.5 meter separations, the default bar on audience scanning and the duty to notify the FAA all come from the FDA's variance form.197

Guidance is what regulators publish without the force of a rule - the FDA's Laser Notices, the FAA's Advisory Circular, the CAA's CAP 736, DGUV Information 203-036.217215229199

Standards are consensus documents, voluntary unless a law adopts them: IEC 60825-1 and its national copies, the technical report IEC TR 60825-3 for displays and shows, ANSI Z136.1 and Z136.10.29228214

Industry practice is the trade's own: ILDA's audience scanning ceilings, its draft Category A standard - 3.0 meters above and 2.5 meters lateral, a 6 W draft maximum per beam, marked "NOT YET AN OFFICIAL ILDA POLICY" - and PLASA's guide.189195228

Quick answers

Do I need a license or variance to run a laser show?
In the US, show lasers above 5 mW of visible output (Class IIIb and IV) need an FDA variance before sale or use, and some states add registration, notice or operator certificates. The UK has no laser show license; general health and safety duties apply.
What is an FDA laser variance and how do you get one?
It is FDA permission to vary from the 5 mW demonstration laser limit in 21 CFR 1040.11(c). The application is Form FDA 3147 with a Laser Light Show Report (Form 3640) and, for manufacturers, a Product Report (Form 3632), emailed to CDRH; shows may begin only after the approval letter.
How high do laser beams have to be above the audience?
The standard conditions on the FDA's variance form keep laser light above Class I limits at least 3.0 m above any surface where the public may stand and 2.5 m to the side or below. Massachusetts sets 10 ft above and 8 ft to the side; German guidance asks that exposure limits hold up to 2.70 m.
Is it illegal to point a laser at a plane, and what is the penalty?
In the US, 18 U.S.C. 39A makes knowingly aiming a laser pointer at an aircraft a federal crime punishable by a fine, up to five years in prison, or both. The FAA cites criminal penalties up to $250,000 and civil fines up to $11,000 per violation.
